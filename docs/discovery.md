## Home

Home shows your library first: recently added albums, your albums, the artists you have the most albums by, what you played lately in DroppedNeedle, Milestone Anniversaries and a Browse Genres grid. These come straight from the database, so the page is up as soon as you open it. Milestone Anniversaries lists library albums turning 10, 20, 25, 30, 40, 50 or 60 this year, oldest first. Your Top Albums are the albums you played most in DroppedNeedle over the last 30 days, once you have played at least three; until then they are your top albums this month from ListenBrainz or Last.fm.

Trending artists (from ListenBrainz, or Last.fm if that is your primary source), popular albums (from ListenBrainz only, since Last.fm has no sitewide album chart), loved tracks, your own genres and your ListenBrainz weekly exploration load in the background and slide in a few seconds later. They are saved, so they survive a restart, and are rebuilt when they are more than five minutes old. The cards asking to connect a download client or Jellyfin show only to admins, who set those up.

Between the charts and your own shelves is the way into Discover: the first pick of your weekly set you have not answered, with its cover, its reason and its place in the week ("Discover - 3 of 8 this week"). Once every pick is answered it says so, with the week's covers and the day the next set is out. While a new set is due but not out yet, it shows last week's next open pick, as Discover shows last week's set. With no set it is not shown. It reads only what the server already keeps, so it never holds Home up. The whole strip opens Discover.

## Discover

The top of Discover is your weekly set, laid out as a record shop counter (see [The weekly set](#the-weekly-set) and [The counter](#the-counter)). Under it are shelves that change on their own:

- **New from your artists**: the last 90 days' releases from artists you play, own or follow.
- **Gaps in artists you collect**: albums missing from artists you play and have three or more albums of.
- **From your shelves**: your own albums played a lot but not lately, and ones you asked for and never played.
- **Daily Mixes** and **Radio Stations**, grown from your artists.
- **Weekly Exploration**, your ListenBrainz weekly playlist, shown only while it is less than a week old, here and on Home.
- **Discover for a Playlist** and **Browse by Genre**.

Then Past weeks, and the page ends with "That is everything this week." and one link, What is popular on ListenBrainz (on Last.fm when that is your primary source), which opens the charts at `/popular`. There is no chart on Discover itself. Settings > Discover switches each shelf on or off, and New set on there chooses your set day.

What left the page: Top Picks and the Listening Lounge (the weekly set took their place), Because You Listened, Artists You Might Like, Popular in Your Genres, Genres to Explore and Globally Trending. Fresh Releases and New From Artists You Follow became New from your artists, Missing Essentials became Gaps in artists you collect and Rediscover became From your shelves. Milestone Anniversaries moved to Home. Recent Scrobbles and your two Last.fm weekly charts moved to Your Top Albums (`/your-top`), under the chart, for a person with Last.fm linked. The upgrade, from an earlier v3 or from v2, carried your switches over: a switch for a renamed shelf keeps its place, Anniversaries' moved to Settings > Home, New from your artists is off only if you had both shelves it replaces off, and a switch for a shelf that left is gone.

### How the page is built

The page is built in the background, so opening it never waits: you see the last page at once, and a page older than an hour is rebuilt while you look at it and swapped in when it is done. The last good page is saved, so it survives a restart, and a library scan never throws it away: albums you add or remove show as owned or not on your next visit. While you keep using Discover, the server rebuilds your page every twelve hours; it stops a day after your last visit. Set `DISCOVER_WARMER_ENABLED=false` to turn that background work off.

A shelf whose reads failed keeps what it last showed from a build that worked, for up to a week, with a line saying which service isn't answering and when those albums are from. With nothing to keep, it shows what the failed build did get and says the service is down. A shelf that has nothing to show, needs a service you haven't linked, came back much shorter than before or hasn't been built yet says so in its place. When a build fails or finds nothing, the next waits five minutes.

When an outside service such as ListenBrainz, Last.fm or MusicBrainz is down or rate limiting, a page that needs it says which service is not answering and when to try again. The API answers `503 PROVIDER_UNAVAILABLE` with the service in `details.provider` and the wait, when the service gave one, in `details.retry_after` and the `Retry-After` header. A service that needs a key first, or refused the saved one, answers `409 PROVIDER_NOT_CONFIGURED`.

Each build writes one line to the server log with its numbers: how many cards it holds, how many sit in shelves about you, how many repeat the last page. An admin can read every user's latest numbers from `GET /api/v3/admin/discover/health`. They are counts only: no album, artist or play is named.

### Your artists

The shelves about you, and your weekly set, grow from your seed artists: who you play in DroppedNeedle (from any app) and in the ListenBrainz history you added, the artists, albums and songs you heart or rate four or five stars, the albums you said you know already, the artists you follow, your own playlists (not the Weekly Mix), the albums you asked for, the albums on your Later pile, the artists you named as ones you love, and, when you linked them, your ListenBrainz and Last.fm top artists and your own Jellyfin's most played and favorite artists. Plays spread over several months count more, and a play counts half as much for every six months since. Two plays (a play here and its scrobble count as one), a heart on the artist or one of their albums, or a follow make an artist a seed; a one or two-star rating counts against one. When that gives too few, the artists your library holds the most albums by add a little weight. Only artists with a MusicBrainz id can be seeds, and Various Artists never is. Nobody else's plays, hearts or requests are used for you, and Jellyfin is read only through your own link. With nothing linked and nothing played, hearted or followed yet, there are no seeds: the shelves say what to link, and the counter asks you to name a few artists you love.

You can name artists you love, any artist on MusicBrainz, owned or not. While Discover has nothing to set out for you, the counter's card holds the field: type a name, choose Add on an artist, and its name tag goes under the field; three are enough for a first set, once what Discover needs to know about them has been read. The same field heads What you told Discover, where removing a tag takes the artist back. Naming an artist is not a follow: nothing is watched or requested. Each one you name is a seed artist, never offered to you as a new artist, and starts out counting as much as your typical seed (as much as a follow while you have no other); it counts half as much for every 50 plays of that artist, so what you play soon outweighs what you said. You can name up to 30. A change sets your next set and dig further to follow it; this week's set stays as it is. The list is yours alone, and Forget my history leaves it.

Similar artists are one list per artist, the same everywhere: Discover, your weekly set, radio, the artist and album pages and the Weekly Mix. It is ListenBrainz's list of artists people play in the same listening sessions, with fame divided out, so an artist does not come first just for being in everyone's history. Last.fm's similar artists stand in when ListenBrainz reads are off or ListenBrainz has nothing for the artist. With both Last.fm and ListenBrainz linked, your seed artists come from both; an admin can turn that off under Settings > Advanced, and Discover then follows your primary source only. The Primary source under Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery decides where album popularity comes from: Last.fm or ListenBrainz.

Where Discover, radio or an album page's "You Might Also Like" offers an artist's albums, it takes them from the artist's discography on MusicBrainz, for everyone, with or without a ListenBrainz link, and offers only albums and EPs credited to that artist: singles, live albums, demos, compilations, soundtracks, remix albums, DJ mixes, spoken word, interviews, audiobooks and audio dramas are left out. They come most listened first, by how many people play them on ListenBrainz, or newest first while ListenBrainz is not giving counts.

Similar artists, listener counts and artists' albums are kept facts: the server keeps them in its database, shared by everyone, so a build never waits on ListenBrainz or MusicBrainz for them. One background job reads what was asked for and refreshes what people still use: similar artists and album lists after 14 days, listener counts after 30. A failed read keeps what was kept and is tried again later. These reads, and every other background read Discover and Home make to ListenBrainz or the official MusicBrainz service, are held to a daily ceiling: 60 plus 30 for each person who used the app in the last seven days, at most 300. BrainzMash and mirrors are not counted. On a new install using the official MusicBrainz service, a person's first album lists can take two days to arrive. Clearing provider data under Settings > Cache clears the kept facts too; they are read again in the background.

### New from your artists

New from your artists lists every release of the last 90 days by an artist you play, your library has an album of, you follow, or that is among the 50 strongest artists Discover found close to yours, newest first, each with its release date and type. A release your library holds is marked Owned, one you asked for says Requested, and the rest have Keep, an album request. One by an artist close to yours says which of yours, as "New from Thom Yorke, close to Radiohead." Discover shows the newest 12, and All opens the whole list at `/discover/new-releases`. The list comes from ListenBrainz's list of every new release, read once a day for everyone with no account, and from the new releases following an artist finds. Soundtracks, remix albums, DJ mixes, spoken records and anything you told Discover to hide are left out. When the day's read fails, the list keeps the one before and says when it is from.

### Gaps in artists you collect

Gaps shows albums missing from the artists you play, heart, rate, follow or ask for that your library holds three or more albums of: the ten such artists that weigh most in your listening, up to three albums each, 15 in all. Each is drawn as the artist's shelf with a hole in it: their albums your library holds in date order, and a dashed space where the missing one's year falls, beside its name, "You have 6 of their albums, not this one." and Keep. Only albums and EPs are offered, never one your library holds or is fetching, one you played, or one you told Discover to hide. You need no ListenBrainz link, and two people on one library see different gaps.

### From your shelves

From your shelves turns over albums your library holds, in two lists of up to eight. Played a lot, not lately lists albums you played eight times or more here but not in the last 90 days, each saying so from its own numbers ("18 plays in February, none since.") with a small gauge, one mark a month. Asked for, never played lists albums you requested that arrived at least 14 days ago and that you have not played since. Only your own plays and requests count, and an album no longer in the library leaves both lists.

### The Later pile

The Later pile keeps albums you want to come back to. Later on a pick, or on the page of an album you don't own, puts it there; choose it again on the album page to take it off. The pile has its own page, `/discover/later`, with the newest 100 albums on it. Each can be sampled, kept or removed, and Keep all opens the Download this section window with up to 30 of the newest albums not yet requested. Nothing on the pile is requested by itself. An album on the pile is not offered again as a pick, its artist counts toward your seed artists as a follow would, and once the library holds it, it leaves the pile. Forget my history leaves the pile.

## The weekly set

Once a week Discover chooses up to eight albums for you from your own list of candidates and keeps them as your set for that week. The week starts at 00:00 server time on your set day: Friday unless you choose another under Settings > Discover (the API has it at `/api/v3/discover/prefs`). The server runs on UTC in the official image, so a Friday set comes out on Thursday afternoon or evening in the Americas. A new set day counts from the first such day after the week you are in.

Your list of candidates is worked out in the background from your artists: albums by artists people who play yours also play, leaving out what your library holds or is fetching, what you told Discover and artists you already know. Only albums found through your own artists go in a set; ListenBrainz's weekly picks for you are in dig further. The eight are spread over your tastes by their share, one album an artist at most, and none repeats an album your sets held in the last twelve weeks. A pick is never a chart album: with fewer than eight to offer the set is short and says why. Each pick shows one line saying why it is there, such as "Close to Radiohead.", and Why this? opens the rest.

The set is built on your set day if you used the app in the last 30 days, or on your first visit to Discover after it; with `DISCOVER_WARMER_ENABLED=false` only on a visit. Building it reads only what the server already keeps, so it asks no outside service. While the albums it needs are still being read it waits and says how many it has found so far; if a service it waits on keeps failing, last week's set stays and the page says which service and since when. Your first set waits for your ListenBrainz history while it is being imported, for six hours at most.

Once built, a set holds still: a new build of your list, something you told Discover or a new set day does not change it. What changes is what you did with each pick. Past the eight, dig further deals the rest of your list the same way, in its order, one album an artist and none by the set's artists. Every week's set is kept until you forget your history.

## Genres

Genre tiles on Home and Discover use covers from your own albums in that genre: up to four, picked from different artists, and only art your library actually has (a cover image in the album folder or one embedded in the files). Genres you have nothing in get a plain color. Click a genre to see the artists and albums you own in it next to the popular ones on MusicBrainz. Your own show their first rows, with "View all" for the rest. The popular ones show three rows; "View more" opens the rest and then pages through them. While you look, the server fetches pictures for those popular artists and albums in the background, slowly, so the page is quicker next time.

## The counter

The header gives the week's number, the day the set came out, how many albums it holds and when the next set comes, with links to your Later pile (while it holds anything), Past weeks and What you told Discover; on a phone those links sit under the bin. On the left of the counter is one pick at a time: its cover with a play button, the album, artist, year and type, and an index card with the line saying why it is there. The tape over the card says New to you, or Picked from your shelves while your ListenBrainz history is not linked, with Further out on a pick not filed under one of your tastes. The arrows, the arrow keys or a swipe on a phone move between picks.

On the right is the bin: the week's picks filed behind a tab for each of your tastes, named "Around" its heaviest artist, with its share of your listening. Each row says what became of its pick: Kept, Later, Know it, Passed, Waiting while the request waits for an admin, Refused when an admin turned it down, or Didn't arrive when it could not be downloaded, with a headphones mark once you heard its sample. Along the front are the listening post, which says what the sampler plays and has its volume on a knob, and the receipt: your plays, your artists, the artists they led to, how many you already know were left out and how many were picked, counted when the set was built.

Keep asks for the album, as Add to Library does elsewhere. Later puts it on your Later pile. No hides it, and for eight seconds a slip offers Undo or one of three other answers: Know it already keeps the album away and counts as something you like; Not this artist keeps every album filed under that artist out of Discover until you undo it; Not now keeps the album away for three months. If your request limit is used up, Keep puts the pick on your Later pile instead and the slip says so, with Undo. On a desktop the keys K, L, X and S keep, put on Later, say no and play the sample. Once the eight are answered the counter goes on dealing from dig further, each pick marked Extra; when those run out it lays the week out and says when the next set comes.

Why this? opens the pick's evidence: the artists of yours that led to it, how often you played each and where the pick stands in that artist's similar artists, each with Less from this; whether it is new to you; how many listeners it has; and how it was found.

### Audition

The play button on a pick's cover plays a sample: three 30-second clips, the album's opening track, its best-known track and a deep cut from about two thirds of the way through. Deezer's ranking says which track is best known; when the clips come from iTunes, which has no ranking, the second track stands in. While it plays, the record slides out of the sleeve and turns and the listening post names the clip and where it comes from. Play the same pick again and it starts at the next clip. Play the week, the key on the listening post, plays the week's picks in order, three clips each, passing over picks you said No to or know already. Keep, Later and No answer the pick that is playing and move on. A sample keeps playing when you leave the page, in the small player in the corner, and a phone's lock screen shows it. A sample is never a play: it is not scrobbled and adds nothing to your history or statistics.

### What you told Discover

What you say is left out wherever Discover recommends: dig further, every shelf made for you on Discover and Home (Gaps in artists you collect and the Weekly Exploration playlist included), radio, playlist suggestions, the similar albums on an album's page, the similar artists on an artist's page, and the albums the Weekly Mix tops up with or requests by itself. Charts, your own listening shown back (such as your Last.fm lists on Your Top Albums), shelves of your library, search and an artist's own albums are not filtered. What you told Discover, at the top of the Discover page, lists everything by kind: artists you love, not this artist, Less from this, not now, know it already and no, each saying how long it lasts, with an Undo. Albums hidden before these answers existed keep the year they had; an admin can shorten that under Settings > Advanced, in the Retention section.

Discover keeps a record, for your account, of the albums it offered you and what came of them: where and why each was offered, when it was on screen, sampled or opened, when you asked for it, when it arrived and how much you played it in its first 30 and 90 days, and what you told Discover about it. An album that was never on screen leaves the record 30 days after it was last offered, and one you saw and did nothing with after 13 months. No page shows the record to you or anyone else apart from Past weeks.

## Past weeks

Every weekly set is kept with what you did with it. Under the rest of the page, Past weeks shows the newest week before the one on the counter as a shelf, with a tally such as "1 kept - 1 later - 1 known - 5 open". All weeks opens every week, newest first. Each album says what became of it: Kept (Waiting, Refused or Didn't arrive while the request waits, was turned down or failed), Later, Know it, Passed, or nothing while you never answered. A kept album stands a little taller and, once played, says how many times; a passed one sits lower. Opening an album shows it as it was that week, with the reason it was shown with.

At the top of All weeks, So far counts every album your sets held and what you did with them. The filters show only what you kept, put on Later, passed or never answered, and the search finds an album by title or artist across every week. An album you kept from a set says "Found on Discover, week 47" in its page's header, with a link to that week. Past weeks are yours alone.

## Your history

What the app keeps about you: your plays here, in the web player and in music apps; a copy of your ListenBrainz history, when you link ListenBrainz or add an export file; the albums Discover offered you and what came of them, and your weekly sets; how each playback ended (see Music apps below); and what you told Discover. Discover also notes which of its pages and which artist pages you opened in the last day, so they stay up to date; that note goes after a day.

When you link ListenBrainz under Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery, the server imports your history by itself, with your own link: it asks ListenBrainz for an export of your listens, waits while ListenBrainz makes it (usually minutes, giving up after a day), downloads it and reads it in. The line under Listening history says "Importing your ListenBrainz history" meanwhile, then how many listens are kept. After that, while you keep using the app, it reads your new listens every six hours. Linking, or opening your Profile or Discover, starts your import at once; for anyone linked and active in the last seven days who has done neither, it starts by itself within two days. Nothing is read for someone who hasn't used the app for a week, nor while an admin has ListenBrainz reads switched off, and these reads count toward the daily ceiling (see Your artists above). A failing read waits six hours, then longer, up to a week. Disconnecting stops the reads and keeps what was imported. Linking a different ListenBrainz account asks first, then replaces the history kept from the old one.

To add your ListenBrainz history from a file instead, download an export of your listens from ListenBrainz (a ZIP file) and choose Add a ListenBrainz export file under Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery. The file can be up to 512 MB. The server reads it in the background, keeps each listen once, leaves out your plays here coming back from ListenBrainz, and deletes the file once it is read. Adding a newer export adds only what is new. An imported listen is never a play here: it changes no play count, scrobble or statistic.

Discover works from a picture of your taste that the server keeps and rebuilds by itself, about once a day while you use the app and soon after your history changes. It weighs every artist you played here or in your ListenBrainz history, with the hearts, ratings, follows, playlists, requests and links that make seed artists, and groups your seed artists by the similar artists the server keeps, each group named "Around" its heaviest artist. Every artist your plays, marks and links name counts as known to you, and so does every album you played here; when your set is chosen, every artist your library has counts as known too. While your ListenBrainz history here is up to date, your top artists and albums are no longer read from ListenBrainz.

To forget it, choose Forget my history under Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery and confirm. It removes the record of the albums Discover offered you, the copy of your ListenBrainz history, how your plays ended, the note of the pages you opened, the picture of your taste, your weekly sets and past weeks, and your saved Discover page and home shelves, which are built again. Your plays and play counts, your library, your requests and discovery batches, what you told Discover, the artists you named, your Later pile and your set day stay. If something stops it part way, choosing it again finishes the rest. Nothing is imported from ListenBrainz again until you add a file or choose Import again. A backup made earlier still holds what it held. Deleting your account removes all of it.

## Radio

Radio plays a station from an artist, an album, a genre or a whole shelf. An artist station uses that artist and up to seven similar ones; a genre station starts from artists in your library tagged with that genre (the same ones all day) and tops up with Last.fm's top artists for the tag. In library mode it plays only your own tracks. In hybrid mode, the default, about half the tracks are popular songs by those artists that you don't own. Songs you don't own play through YouTube when it is set up and are skipped otherwise. No artist gets more than four tracks, and never three in a row. Playing a shelf plays its albums in order: your own tracks where you have the album, and its first few songs where you don't. Radio shelves of albums work the same way: similar artists' albums for an artist, similar albums and more by the same artist for an album. They follow ListenBrainz, or Last.fm while ListenBrainz is having trouble, and need one of the two linked for your account.

## Playlist suggestions

"Suggest Albums" on a playlist's page lists albums to add: albums by artists similar to the ones on it, plus albums in their genres. Artists already on the playlist are left out. It works for your own playlists and public ones. Suggestions start from the artists' MusicBrainz ids. A playlist imported from Jellyfin, Navidrome or Plex doesn't carry those, so each track's artist is looked up in your library: by the track's MusicBrainz recording id, or else by the artist's name when exactly one library artist linked to MusicBrainz has that name. Tracks whose artist isn't found that way don't shape the suggestions. When there is nothing to suggest, the window says why.

## Download all

"Download all" on a Discover shelf, and Keep all on your Later pile, create a discovery batch: every album on it that you don't have and nobody has asked for yet becomes a normal album request. Your quota and the approval rules apply as they would for one album, so a plain user's batch waits for an admin, and a batch that would go over your quota is refused as a whole. Albums you already own or that are already requested are listed as skipped. A batch holds up to 30 albums. Your batches are listed with how many albums have arrived and how many are still pending. Removing a batch withdraws your pending requests from it; if someone else asked for the same album, their request carries on. When an admin removes a batch, the albums it brought in also go to the recycle bin, unless another user asked for, favorited or played them. It never touches albums that were already yours or already requested. If a step fails, that album stays in the batch so you can try again. While no recycle bin is known (its folder can't be reached, say), albums stay in the batch the same way and the page says why. You can also remove just the batch and keep the albums. Pages can send an `idempotency_key` (a UUID) when creating a batch, so a retried "Download all" never files it twice.

## Weekly Mix

Your Weekly Mix is a playlist built for each user with a linked ListenBrainz account, while ListenBrainz recommendations are on. It takes your ListenBrainz weekly-jams and weekly-exploration playlists and tops them up to 100 tracks with the most played song of artists similar to the ones in them, read with your own ListenBrainz link. Without a link there is no Weekly Mix. Songs you already own play from your library. The server rebuilds every mix once a day if it is more than six days old, and you can rebuild yours any time with **Refresh my mix** under Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery. If you switch on **Auto-request missing music from Your Weekly Mix** there, each build also requests up to five albums from the mix you don't have yet. Admins and trusted users get this straight away; other users wait for an admin to approve it on the Requests page. An admin can reject or later revoke it, which also switches the toggle off. The app rebuilds your Weekly Mix by itself, and importing a Spotify playlist again replaces its songs, so changes that people you let edit these playlists make are replaced at the next rebuild or import.

## Covers and artist pictures

Album covers come from your own files first: an image in the album folder (`cover.jpg`, `folder.png`, `front.jpg` and the like) or a picture embedded in a track. An image in a folder that holds tracks of albums with more than one title is not used for any of them, so each takes the picture embedded in its own files, if it has one. The library picks these up after each scan, and large pictures are scaled down for the grid. Albums without local art get their cover from the Cover Art Archive, cached on disk. To prefer the Cover Art Archive over your files, turn off "Prefer local cover art" under Settings > Library > Covers.

Artist pictures come from TheAudioDB. When TheAudioDB has none, DroppedNeedle finds the artist's Wikidata entry by its MusicBrainz id and uses the Deezer picture that entry links to, or else its Wikimedia Commons photo. The match is always by exact id, never by name. A Commons photo shows its author and license in a small line on the artist page, linked to the file's Commons page. The first time an artist shows up the picture is fetched in the background, so a grid of new artists fills in over a few seconds instead of holding the page up. Pictures are kept on disk with the covers. AudioDB album thumbnails stand in for albums that have no cover anywhere else. A library artist that is not linked to MusicBrainz shows a mosaic of their own albums' covers (art in the album's files): four of them in a 2x2, two crosswise when there are only two or three, or the one cover when that is all there is. Albums under their name come first, then albums not tagged as compilations, then the newest. Subsonic and Jellyfin apps show the same picture.

A song in a playlist shows its picture only when it comes from DroppedNeedle itself or from Spotify, Deezer, Last.fm, TheAudioDB or YouTube; any other picture address is dropped and the song shows the placeholder, so nobody can add a picture that tracks who views a public or shared playlist.

When the Cover Art Archive or another picture source cannot be reached, the cover keeps loading rather than showing as missing, and it is asked for again about 30 seconds later. Only an answer from the source that it has no art shows the placeholder.

The server also fetches everything for your library ahead of time: two minutes after a library scan finishes, a library image refresh starts on its own (unless one is already running). An admin can start one any time with "Refresh library images" under Settings > Cache. It goes through your artists (page details and pictures), their top tracks, your albums (details and covers) and finally anything TheAudioDB has not been asked about yet. Their similar artists it leaves to the kept facts' background job. It skips what is already cached, so running it again picks up where the last run stopped. A progress pill at the bottom of the page shows where it is; admins and trusted users can stop it from there. Settings > Cache shows the cached provider answers per source, the kept facts, the cached images and the database size, with a line giving the image cache's size limit and whether it follows the library (automatic) or is set by `COVER_CACHE_MAX_SIZE_MB`, and clears the provider data or the images when you want a fresh start. Clearing the provider answers also drops the kept facts and everyone's built Discover page and home shelves; each is built again on its next visit.

## Last.fm and ListenBrainz

Last.fm works like this: the admin registers one Last.fm API application at last.fm/api/account/create, saves its key and shared secret under Settings > Last.fm and switches Last.fm on, once for the whole server. From 1 December 2026 Last.fm accepts a key only while the account that owns it has Last.fm Pro: the admin's account for the server's key, the user's for their own. Without Pro, Last.fm refuses the key and its features stop for everyone using it. "Test connection" there checks the pair before or after saving it and says which one Last.fm turned down: an invalid key, a suspended key, or a shared secret that does not match the key. Each user then clicks Connect under Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery, approves DroppedNeedle on last.fm, and clicks Finish. Their scrobbles go to their own Last.fm account. Users can also bring their own API application: if the server has no key saved, Connect asks for the user's own key and secret first, and a user can always store a pair through the API (`PUT /api/v3/me/connections/lastfm`). A user's own pair replaces the server's key for that user only. The Last.fm sections on artist and album pages follow the same rule: they read with the user's own key when one is saved, and with the server's key otherwise. Last.fm charts on the home page read with your own key when you saved one, and the server's key otherwise; "your top albums" from Last.fm read your linked account. With Last.fm linked, Your Top Albums (`/your-top`) also shows your recent scrobbles and this week's top albums and artists from Last.fm, read when you open the page. Last.fm names albums by release, so each is matched to its MusicBrainz release group (remembered answers first, then at most a few lookups); one not matched yet shows Last.fm's cover and no link. If the admin later changes the server's key, everyone who linked with the old one has to click Connect again: Last.fm sessions belong to the key that created them. Link ListenBrainz with the token from your ListenBrainz profile. Scrobbles are saved with the play: if Last.fm or ListenBrainz is down or slow, or the server restarts, they are sent later, as long as the play is less than two weeks old. Turning forwarding off, or unlinking, stops the ones still waiting, and so does a service refusing your token; link again to scrobble new plays. When the admin switches Last.fm off, the Last.fm scrobbles still waiting are dropped, and Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery says "Last.fm is off on this server." instead of offering Connect, with Last.fm scrobbling off and Home and Discover following ListenBrainz and no longer offering a Connect Last.fm card (the API answers `409 LASTFM_OFF`; a missing key pair answers `409 PROVIDER_NOT_CONFIGURED`). Artist pages show photos from AudioDB, Deezer and Wikimedia Commons (AudioDB is on by default, free key rate limits apply), with the AudioDB key and whether your browser loads its images directly or through DroppedNeedle under Settings > Advanced. Artist thumbnails across the app use the same pictures.

An artist page's popular songs and popular albums come from the listening service you chose, and the albums in an album page's "You Might Also Like" from your primary source. Similar artists, on both pages, are the one list described under Discover, whichever service you chose. When a service is switched off, not set up, has nothing or cannot be reached, the other one fills in, either way round, and on an artist page a line above the lists says so.

ListenBrainz reads are on unless an admin turns them off under Settings > ListenBrainz. Discover's weekly set and shelves are made from what the server keeps about you (see [Your history](#your-history)): your plays here, your marks and follows, and your ListenBrainz history once you link ListenBrainz or add an export file, so linking adds your listening elsewhere to your picks. Album and artist pages, Discover and the listen counts shown on search results read ListenBrainz with your own ListenBrainz link when you have one, and without an account otherwise. Library Management's genres always go out without one, and so do the counts search sorts by: with ListenBrainz as your primary source, well-matched albums and songs are ordered by how often people play them, using counts already fetched, and missing ones are fetched in the background for your next search (about two ListenBrainz calls for a new search, kept 30 days), so search never waits on ListenBrainz. Last.fm followers' searches ask ListenBrainz nothing, though their similar artists still come from ListenBrainz. ListenBrainz shows an artist's popular albums and songs only to a linked account, so without your own link the server does not ask it for the artist page's lists. They then come from Last.fm when you have it, and from what is already fetched for other people. Otherwise an artist page says to link ListenBrainz under Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery. The albums Discover, radio and album pages offer from an artist need no link, and nobody's link is used for them. That is not an outage, so no "unavailable" warning shows for it. The server never reads with an account of its own. With the switch off, nothing in the app reads ListenBrainz: Discover, Home, radio, Your Weekly Mix and Wrapped included. Shelves then come from Last.fm where it is set up, and say "Recommendations are turned off." where nothing else can answer. Linking your own ListenBrainz account and scrobbling to it work either way.

A popular song that names no album, as Last.fm's don't, is looked up on MusicBrainz in the background the first time the artist's page opens (at most 20 lookups a page, kept for a week), so it shows its album's cover from the next visit. A popular song you have on one of the artist's own albums, or on the album it is shown with, shows that album and plays your copy.

## Music apps

Music apps (Subsonic and Jellyfin) sign in with an app password you make under Profile > Connect Apps, or, for apps an admin has listed, through the browser ([Signing in apps from the browser](app-connect.md)). Your apps list your own playlists and those shared with you, and you can add, remove and reorder songs in a shared one when its owner lets you edit it. An app shows only the songs your library holds, and its edits leave the others (songs still waiting for a download) where they are. The switch under Profile > Connect Apps, off by default for everyone, adds other people's public playlists to those lists. It changes only what apps list: an app that already holds a public playlist's id can still open it with the switch off, a private playlist never opens for anyone it isn't shared with, and the web UI is unaffected. Some apps, such as Music Assistant, ask for every song's lyrics each time they sync your library: lyrics from your files always come, but an app looks up at most 60 songs' lyrics online an hour for you (the web player has no such limit), and the rest come on a later sync. What you play in an app counts like a play in the web player: it shows in the app's recently played and most played lists, with play counts and last-played times. A play in the web player counts, whether or not you scrobble, once you have heard half the track or four minutes of it (tracks under 30 seconds never count). A play through a connected server, a radio YouTube pick, a YouTube track link or an imported playlist entry counts as your library's track when it carries that track's MusicBrainz recording id. A YouTube link takes that id from the album's tracklist, so it says which track the link stands for, not what the video holds. A full-album video carries none, and neither does a playlist entry imported by an older version or moved to another source. The id also goes to ListenBrainz with these plays when you forward them. Playing a Jellyfin, Navidrome or Plex track in the web player also reports it to that server under your own linked account (Profile > Media Server Accounts), so it shows there as it would from that server's own apps: in its Now Playing, play counts and recently played. Without a linked account of your own nothing is reported there. **Now Playing visibility** under Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery sets how your listening appears to others: **Show what I'm playing**, **Show I'm listening, hide the track** or **Don't show my activity**. It covers library and YouTube tracks played in the web player, and what you play in Subsonic and Jellyfin apps that connect to DroppedNeedle. Sessions the server picks up from Jellyfin, Navidrome or Plex always show in full, whichever option you pick. It does not change what Last.fm or ListenBrainz receive. Navidrome counts the play once you have heard half the track or four minutes of it, and passes it on to Last.fm or ListenBrainz when you linked them inside Navidrome. Leave "Let Navidrome handle Last.fm and ListenBrainz" on under Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery in that case; with it off, such a play is scrobbled twice. Your DroppedNeedle history holds one play per listen either way. Previews never count. The server also keeps, for you, how each playback ended: what played, how long you heard it (for a music app, the last position it reported), whether it finished or was skipped, stopped or failed, and what it was started from, such as an album, a playlist or a station. This record is apart from your plays and never changes a play count, a scrobble or a statistic. Marking a song, album or artist played or unplayed in a Jellyfin app is kept for you alone. Marking an album marks each of its songs, marking an artist marks the songs on the albums they lead, and an album counts as played once all its songs are. Unplayed hides your earlier plays from your Jellyfin apps' played mark and play count, and leaves your history, statistics, Discover and Subsonic apps as they were. A Subsonic app needs to report only when playback starts, pauses, resumes or seeks: you show as playing until the track would end, plus 45 seconds, and a pause for 45 seconds. Last.fm and ListenBrainz hear that a track is playing once per track, again only when it repeats or after a stop, and never for a pause. Jellyfin apps report every few seconds and drop out 45 seconds after their last report. Subsonic apps get each album's and artist's MusicBrainz ids, and Jellyfin apps each album's and track's, from DroppedNeedle's match, or, before identification has reached the album, the ids the files' tags agree on (an album whose match was taken off, or that identification found nothing for, sends none); tag ids are passed to apps only and never become an album's or artist's identity. A track is only transcoded when the app asks for another format or a lower bitrate. If the app names no bitrate, Opus streams at 128 kbps and MP3 at 192 kbps, never above the max bitrate you set. A bitrate asked for below 64 kbps streams at 64 kbps. Each user can run two transcodes at once, so gapless players can load the next track early. A transcode an app has not read for 30 minutes is stopped to free its slot, and the track may need playing again. Finamp's transcoded playback uses HLS, which needs transcoding on and ffmpeg installed. Only sign-in attempts are rate limited per IP address. A run of wrong passwords from one address locks it out for a short while. A device repeating one wrong password counts once a minute and is held to about five tries a second, and an app still using a revoked app password does not count at all, so neither can lock out other apps on the same network. A Subsonic app that signs in with a token and salt counts every try, as each try looks new. Album and artist pictures in Subsonic apps come from signed links under `/subsonic/img` that open without signing in, so an app's picture loader can show them; playlist covers and music always need sign-in. A link is the same for every user and does not expire. It stops working when the album or artist leaves the library, while the Subsonic API is off, or if the server's data key is replaced, and the app gets a new link the next time it reads the album or artist. A changed album cover reaches apps the next time they read the album, and Finamp within a day. A changed artist picture can take longer to reach apps. Jellyfin apps load album and artist pictures without signing in too, as they would from a real Jellyfin server, but only by ids that this server alone can make: album and artist ids are made with the server's data key, so nobody without an account can work one out or test whether an album is in your library. Song pictures come from the song's album. Every id handed out is stored, so apps keep their pins and downloads across restarts. A stored album or artist id survives a replaced `config/data_enc.key` (`droppedneedle-tool reset-key`); only items added afterwards get ids under the new key. Whoever holds an old key can still work out the picture ids of albums and artists stored before it was replaced.

## Spotify playlist import

Spotify playlist import needs an app from the Spotify developer dashboard. Add the redirect URI that Settings > Spotify shows (it ends in `/api/v3/acquire/spotify/auth/callback`) to the app, then save the app's client ID and secret there. With "Redirect Origin" left empty, that address is the one DroppedNeedle works out for itself, which is right when you reach it directly or through a reverse proxy listed in `TRUSTED_PROXY_IPS` that sends `X-Forwarded-Proto`; if it is not the address in your browser's address bar, fill in Redirect Origin. Each user then clicks Connect on the Spotify card in their profile, and Disconnect there unlinks it again. In development mode, a Spotify app allows up to 5 users: add each one by email under **User Management** in the dashboard before they click Connect. An app set up for v2 lists v2's address, which v3 does not answer: add the v3 one in the dashboard (see [Upgrading from v2](upgrading-from-v2.md#before-you-upgrade)).

The list shows the playlists you own and the collaborative playlists you have joined. Spotify gives the songs of any other playlist only to its owner and collaborators, so a playlist you only follow is left out. A collaborative playlist you follow but have not joined still shows, as Spotify's list does not tell the two apart; importing it fails and says Spotify only lets the server read playlists you own or have joined.

An import runs in the background, one at a time across all users, and picks up again after a restart. Each album on the playlist is matched to its MusicBrainz release group only when MusicBrainz lists one of the album's track ISRCs on a release under the album's title. A multi-track release MusicBrainz lists no ISRCs for at all counts instead when its title, one of its artists, its track count (at least two tracks) and its type are the album's, and every song of the album on the playlist is on it; one-track singles still need an ISRC match, and a release that lists other ISRCs never counts that way. An album that can't be proved either way is left unmatched rather than guessed by name, and it is looked up again after a day. The ISRCs are looked up first, so every edition counts and a soundtrack or compilation is found as well as an artist's album. When the same recording is on an album and a single of the same name, Spotify's album type and then its track count say which one it is.

Once an import finishes, each song whose matched album is in your library plays from the library's copy: the track at the same disc and track number when its title agrees, else the track with the same title elsewhere on that album, else the track at that place when the titles differ only by a "(feat. ...)" credit, "&" against "and", "/" spacing or apostrophes. Songs on an album that comes in later link the same way a few seconds after it lands, however it arrives (a download, the import folder, a scan, or an identification that names the album later), with no need to open the playlist. Those songs then also show in Subsonic and Jellyfin apps, which list only songs with a library file.

### Liked Songs

Your Liked Songs are the first tile on the Spotify page, and import into a playlist of their own, newest at the top. A Spotify link made before DroppedNeedle could read Liked Songs needs one more sign-in: the tile and the Spotify card on your profile say so and offer Reconnect.

### Keep in sync

A playlist imported from Spotify, and Liked Songs, can be kept in step with Spotify: its owner turns on Keep in sync on the playlist page. Once an hour DroppedNeedle asks Spotify whether the playlist changed and reads its songs when it did, and reads a playlist in full once a day in any case (Liked Songs has no daily full read). New songs go at the end (for Liked Songs, at the top). Sync only adds: a song taken off on Spotify stays, nothing is reordered, and a song you removed here is not added again. While a playlist is synced, the Spotify page marks it Synced instead of offering Re-import. People you let edit the playlist see the sync's status line but not its switches.

New songs the library doesn't have are requested by themselves, as the playlist owner's requests, so they count toward the owner's quota and wait while it is full. The Request choice beside the switch says what is asked for: Songs, or Whole albums, which requests each new song's album instead of the song. A song whose request was made once is never asked for again, however that request ended. Admins and trusted users need no approval. A regular user's sync asks an admin once, under Requests > Auto-downloads; until then, and after an admin rejects it or takes the approval back, songs are still added and get their albums, and nothing is requested. On a collaborative Spotify playlist, the songs other people add are requested as the owner's, under the owner's approval and quota.

A song's album is found the way an import finds it, by proof and never by name. A song nothing proves yet shows "No album found yet" and is looked for again 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days later, then every 30 days, and is never requested until an album is found. When Spotify files a song on a compilation (its type says so, its album artist is "Various Artists", or MusicBrainz knows that title only as compilations or soundtracks), the request goes to the artist's own album, EP or single that MusicBrainz shows the recording on, albums first. The row then says "Spotify lists it on" the compilation, and the owner's "Use Spotify's album" in the row menu puts it back there for good and cancels the request made for the other album while it still waits, nobody else asked for it and it covers no other song on the playlist. A Spotify album wrongly typed as a compilation can move a song to its single this way; "Use Spotify's album" undoes that too.

Sync goes at a steady pace: each pass requests at most 25 songs, looks up at most 25 albums and checks at most 25 songs with MusicBrainz before requesting them, so a large playlist or Liked Songs catches up over days, with "Requesting missing songs" and how many are left on the playlist. Once caught up, a sync costs MusicBrainz about 10 to 30 lookups a day for a typical install; songs the library already holds cost none. A large first catch-up costs more: about one lookup per missing song, plus up to a few for an album MusicBrainz has no ISRC for. A 3,000-song Liked Songs catches up over about five days at about 600 a day on average, and up to about 2,000 on the first days.

Liked Songs has no version for Spotify to compare, so each pass reads it newest first and stops at the first song already taken. A song unliked on Spotify while that read runs can, rarely, hide one new song from it.

## Playlist files

A playlist's menu exports it as M3U (extended M3U8) or CSV, and "Import playlist" on the Playlists page reads one of those files back into a new private playlist of yours, named after the file.

An export lists each entry's file by its path inside its library folder, such as `Portishead/Dummy/01 Mysterons.flac`. If your player keeps music under a folder of its own, add a path prefix (`Music/`, say) and it goes before every path; the export window remembers it in this browser. Entries with no file in your library (a Spotify or YouTube entry, a removed track) are left out, and the export says how many. A CSV has a header row and the columns `title`, `artist`, `album`, `album artist`, `duration` (whole seconds) and `path`. A cell that starts with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a carriage return gets a `'` in front, so a spreadsheet shows it as text; the import takes that `'` off again.

An import takes `.m3u`, `.m3u8` and `.csv` files of up to 2 MB. A CSV may have its columns in any order and other columns besides, as long as its header names at least one of the columns above; without such a header the export's column order is assumed. The import also reads the column names common Spotify exporters write (`Track Name`, `Artist Name(s)`, `Album Name` and the like). A file that starts with a byte-order mark is read the same as one without. Each entry is matched to a track in your library, in this order:

1. its path, exactly as the library holds it;
2. the end of its path, from a folder boundary, so a prefix such as `Music/` or `D:\Music\` doesn't matter;
3. its title, artist and album, ignoring capitals, accents and spacing;
4. its title and artist the same way;
5. if none of those finds it and its artist cell names several artists, such as `A, B` or `A; B`, its title, the first of them and its album, the same way.

Each library track is added once, in the file's order. An entry the library doesn't have, but that names a title and an artist, is kept as a song with no file, so you can request it; switch on "Only songs already in the library" before importing to leave those out instead. A CSV's `ISRC` column and an M3U's `#EXTALB:` lines are read for those songs. The import reports how many tracks it added, how many songs it kept to request, and each entry it left out, with its title, artist and why (the page lists the first 200; the API answer holds them all): it names no file, nothing in your library matches it, or the same track came earlier in the file. It reads the first 10,000 entries; any past that are only counted, and the report says how many.

### Kept songs and Request missing

After an import that kept songs, the server looks up each kept song's album on MusicBrainz in the background, by proof and never by name alone: by the song's ISRC on a release with the album's title, or, without one (or when MusicBrainz doesn't know it), by every song filed under that album and artist, up to five, being on an official release of two or more tracks of that title by that artist, all in one album. When the songs fit more than one album, or none, the song shows "No album found yet" and isn't requested. A found album links the song to your library once that album lands, as other playlist songs do. If MusicBrainz is down, the lookup waits and tries again for about three days.

The owner and anyone the playlist is shared with to edit see what each missing song waits for (Awaiting approval, Downloading, Awaiting review, Rejected, Album in library) and a line above the songs saying how many albums there are to request, with a button such as "Request 3 albums". It asks, as you, for the whole albums of the songs whose album was found, under your own approval and quota. Albums already in the library, already asked for, or rejected before are skipped. On a synced playlist whose requests go ahead, songs the sync is about to request show Queued and are left out of the count and the press; a song the sync tried and could not request is counted again. One press asks for at most 200 albums, and a regular user's press asks for what their quota has room for; press again later for the rest. A song's menu has Request album, for its album alone, and Request just this song. "Album in library" means the album is in your library but this song isn't on it; Request just this song asks for the song. A song saved from YouTube links to your library's copy the same way once its album is in, and plays that copy: its source shows Local. A regular user's press waits for approval as one card (see [Approvals](downloads.md#approvals)).

## Song links

Paste a song link from Deezer, Apple Music, Spotify or YouTube into search, a Deezer short link (`link.deezer.com`), or a song.link address made from a Deezer, Apple Music or Spotify link, and search shows that song: it plays when your library has it, and you can request it when it doesn't. The clipboard button at the end of the search box pastes and searches in one tap, and on Android, once DroppedNeedle is [installed as an app](install-as-an-app.md), Share > DroppedNeedle in another app opens the search with the link.

A song link searched from a Subsonic app that searches on the server finds your library's copy of that song in the app's song results; a song you don't have yet shows nothing there.

Deezer and Spotify links are matched by the song's ISRC, and every MusicBrainz recording filed under that ISRC is shown. Apple Music links give no ISRC, so the song is matched by title and artist, and only a close match is shown: a high MusicBrainz score and a length within three seconds of the store's. A YouTube link is matched by its video's title, read as "Artist - Title" with tags such as "(Official Video)" dropped, or by the channel for an auto-generated "Artist - Topic" video. YouTube gives no length, so a remix or live video can miss. A Deezer short link is followed one step, and works when it leads to a song. When nothing matches, search says so rather than guess.

Spotify links need Spotify set up by an admin under Settings > Spotify: they are read with that app, without anyone's sign-in. While Spotify is not set up or is switched off, search says so. YouTube links work while an admin has YouTube switched on.

Each new link pasted costs one call to its service (two for a Deezer short link) and one MusicBrainz search. Spotify also hands out a token, which is reused until it expires, about an hour. A YouTube link is read through YouTube's embed information, which spends none of the daily YouTube quota. Both answers are kept for a day, so pasting the same link again asks nothing.

On an iPhone, a shortcut puts DroppedNeedle in the share sheet. In the Shortcuts app, make a shortcut, turn on Show in Share Sheet and let it receive URLs and text. Add three actions: URL Encode with the Shortcut Input, a Text action holding your server's address, then `/search?q=`, then the encoded text, and Open URLs with that text.
