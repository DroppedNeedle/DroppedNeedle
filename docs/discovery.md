## Home

The Home page shows your library first: recently added albums, your albums, the artists you have the most albums by, what you played lately in DroppedNeedle, and a Browse Genres grid. Those come straight from the database, so the page is up as soon as you open it. Your Top Albums are the albums you played most in DroppedNeedle over the last 30 days, with your play counts, once you have played at least three; until then they are your top albums this month from ListenBrainz or Last.fm. Trending artists and popular albums (from ListenBrainz, or Last.fm if that is your primary source), loved tracks, your own genres and your ListenBrainz weekly exploration load in the background and slide in a few seconds later. Those shelves are saved, so they survive a restart, and are quietly rebuilt when they are more than five minutes old. Popular Right Now shows each album once. If you also use Discover, Home shows a teaser from your Discover page. The cards asking to connect a download client or Jellyfin show only to admins, who set those up.

## Discover

The Discover page fills its shelves from your listening history: top picks for you, "because you listen to" rows, artists you might like, fresh releases, albums missing from your favorite artists, artists to rediscover from your own Jellyfin plays, popular albums in your genres, daily mixes, radio stations, your ListenBrainz weekly exploration (shown only while it is less than a week old, here and on Home) and what listeners like you play, anniversaries and new releases from artists you follow, plus global trends and your Last.fm weekly charts when Last.fm is linked. Top picks come only from artists like your seed artists, never from a chart: with nothing to go on there are none, and the shelf says why. Albums you own are marked. Where Discover, the discover queue, radio or an album page's "You Might Also Like" offers an artist's albums, it takes them from the artist's discography on MusicBrainz, for everyone, with or without a ListenBrainz link, and offers only albums and EPs credited to that artist: singles, live albums, demos, compilations and albums the artist only appears on are left out. They come most listened first, by how many people play them on ListenBrainz, which is asked without an account; while ListenBrainz reads are off or it is not giving counts, they come newest first. For an artist with more than 100 release groups on MusicBrainz, nothing is offered until the rest of the list has been read in the background, so its albums show from a later build. The artist page's own popular albums still list everything. The page is built in the background, so opening it never waits: you see the last page right away, and a page older than an hour rebuilds while you look at it and swaps in when it is done. What the shelves are built from is kept between builds: your listening statistics for six hours, your genres, fresh releases and the sitewide charts for twelve, popularity for up to 14 days, which album a recording belongs to for up to 30 days, and your latest scrobbles and loved tracks for 15 minutes. Similar artists, listener counts and artists' albums are kept facts (see below). So a new album can take up to two weeks to show in an artist's popular albums. These answers are kept across upgrades. An admin who wants them sooner can clear the provider answers under Settings > Cache. The Refresh button starts a fresh build from what is kept; it does not read the kept facts again. The last good page is saved, so it survives a restart, and a library scan never throws it away: albums you add or remove show as owned or not on your next visit, without a rebuild. The provider shelves on the home page are saved the same way. While you keep using Discover, the server rebuilds your page and your discover queue every twelve hours in the background; it stops a day after your last visit. When a build fails or finds nothing, as while ListenBrainz is down, the next one waits five minutes, and the next background build 15 minutes. A shelf whose own reads failed keeps what it last showed from a build that worked, for up to a week, less anything a newly built shelf already shows, with a line saying which service isn't answering and when those albums are from. With nothing to keep, it shows whatever the failed build did get, with a line saying the service is down. A shelf that has nothing to show, needs a service you haven't linked, came back much shorter than before or hasn't been built yet says so in its place. Set `DISCOVER_WARMER_ENABLED=false` to turn that background work off. How many top picks you get and how much your genres weigh in them are under Settings > Advanced.

The shelves about you, and the discover queue, grow from your seed artists: who you play in DroppedNeedle (from any app) and in the ListenBrainz history you added (plays spread over several months count more, and a play counts half as much for every six months since), the artists, albums and songs you heart or rate four or five stars, the albums you said you know already (each counts as a heart on the album), the artists you follow, your own playlists (not the Weekly Mix), the albums you asked for yourself, and, when you linked them, your ListenBrainz top artists of this year (and of all time when this year has too few; not read while your ListenBrainz history here is up to date, since it already holds them), your Last.fm top artists and your own Jellyfin's most played and favorite artists. Two recent plays, a heart on the artist or one of their albums, or a follow make an artist a seed; one play or one hearted song is not enough on its own, and a one or two-star rating counts against an artist. When that gives too few, the artists your library holds the most albums by add a little weight. Only artists with a MusicBrainz id can be seeds, and Various Artists never is. Everything here is your own: nobody else's plays, hearts or requests are used for you, and Jellyfin is read only through your own Jellyfin link, never through an account an admin set up for the whole server. A "because you listen to" row shows how many times you played that artist in DroppedNeedle. With nothing linked and nothing played, hearted or followed yet, there are no seeds: those shelves say what to link, and the queue is trending albums, as it is when nothing like your seeds turns up.

Daily mixes pick their starting artists once a week, from your library, and shuffle what they pick from those artists every day. The Primary source under Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery decides where album popularity comes from: with Last.fm as your primary source, Discover, Missing Essentials, radio, search counts and the library image refresh read Last.fm, apart from the sitewide album chart, which only ListenBrainz has, and similar artists, which are ListenBrainz's lists and listener counts for everyone. If Last.fm refuses your key, these go back to ListenBrainz for an hour at a time. The queue's cards still show ListenBrainz listen counts, and radio takes the name of an artist outside your library from the artist's albums, read from MusicBrainz and counted by ListenBrainz. Last.fm names albums by release, so matching one costs one or two MusicBrainz calls the first time, at most 30 calls per build; albums not yet matched appear with a later build.

Similar artists are one list per artist, the same everywhere: Discover's shelves, the queue, radio, the artist and album pages and the Weekly Mix. It is ListenBrainz's list of the artists people play in the same listening sessions, with fame divided out, so an artist does not come first just for being in everyone's history, and one ListenBrainz has no listener count for comes last. It is used for everyone while ListenBrainz reads are on, Last.fm users included. Last.fm's similar artists stand in when ListenBrainz reads are off or ListenBrainz has nothing for the artist, for people who can read Last.fm. If ListenBrainz fails to answer, the kept list stays, and after a failed read the server waits ten minutes (an hour if ListenBrainz has retired the list) before asking for another. With both Last.fm and ListenBrainz linked, Discover's seed artists come from both, artists both name first, and those shelves carry both badges. An admin can turn this off under Settings > Advanced, and Discover then follows your primary source only.

Similar artists, each artist's listener count and each artist's albums are kept facts: the server keeps them in its database, shared by everyone, so building a page never waits on ListenBrainz or MusicBrainz for them. A build that finds one missing shows the rest, keeps what that shelf showed before, and asks for it. One background job reads what was asked for, then refreshes what people still use: similar artists and album lists after 14 days, listener counts after 30. An artist ListenBrainz knows nothing about is asked again after a day, and an artist with no album or EP to offer after 14 days. Opening an artist page nobody has asked about reads its similar artists straight away. A failed read keeps what was kept and is tried again later, waiting longer each time. These background reads, and every other background read Discover and Home make to ListenBrainz or to the official MusicBrainz service, are held to a daily ceiling: 60 plus 30 for each person who used the app in the last seven days, at most 300. BrainzMash and mirrors are not counted. When nobody has used the app for seven days the job stops, and it starts again with the next visit. On a new install using the official MusicBrainz service, a person's first album lists can take two days to arrive. With ListenBrainz reads off nothing is read from ListenBrainz, and the kept lists still answer. Clearing provider data under Settings > Cache clears the kept facts too, and they are read again in the background; the count of reads already made that day stays. A fact nobody has read for 180 days is dropped.

Each Discover page and queue build writes one line to the server log with its numbers: how many cards it holds, how many sit in shelves that are about you, how many of those are chart fill, how many repeat the last page, and the like. An admin can read every user's latest numbers from `GET /api/v3/admin/discover/health`. They are counts only: no album, artist or play is named.

When an outside service such as ListenBrainz, Last.fm or MusicBrainz is down or rate limiting, a page that needs it says which service is not answering and when to try again. The API answers `503 PROVIDER_UNAVAILABLE` with the service in `details.provider` and the wait, when the service gave one, in `details.retry_after` and the `Retry-After` header. A service that needs a key first, or refused the saved one, answers `409 PROVIDER_NOT_CONFIGURED`. The home charts are kept for twelve hours, and your own top albums for six. On the Your Top Albums page, Last.fm albums are matched to MusicBrainz the same way as Discover's, so albums you own are marked and an album Last.fm lists under two releases shows once; one not matched yet shows Last.fm's cover and no link. When ListenBrainz is not answering and Last.fm works for you, trending artists and your top albums come from Last.fm instead, and while ListenBrainz switches album popularity off or rate limits it, Discover takes popularity from Last.fm for a while.

## Genres

Genre tiles on Home and Discover use covers from your own albums in that genre: up to four, picked from different artists, and only art your library actually has (a cover image in the album folder or one embedded in the files). Genres you have nothing in get a plain color. Click a genre to see the artists and albums you own in it next to the popular ones on MusicBrainz. Your own show their first rows, with "View all" for the rest. The popular ones show three rows; "View more" opens the rest and then pages through them. While you look, the server fetches pictures for those popular artists and albums in the background, slowly, so the page is quicker next time.

## The discover queue

The discover queue on the Discover page deals you albums one at a time. It is built in the background from your seed artists (see Discover above): artists similar to them, your genres, fresh releases, artists you love on ListenBrainz and lesser-played albums by your favorites, with a set number of trending picks mixed in (two by default, under Settings > Advanced). Last.fm users get albums and charts from Last.fm instead. Without seed artists, or when nothing like them turns up, the queue is trending albums. Albums already in your library are left out before the cards are dealt, and a queue short of picks stays short rather than filling up with trending albums. Scrolling away from the queue and back keeps your place; it is checked again against your library at most every five minutes. Choose No on an album in the queue or in Top Picks and it goes at once. For eight seconds a line offers Undo, or one of three other answers: Know it already keeps the album away and is kept as something you like; Not this artist keeps every album filed under that artist out of Discover until you undo it (an album filed under someone else that only credits them still shows); Not now keeps the album away for three months. No and Know it already last until you undo them. What you said is left out wherever Discover recommends: the queue, every shelf made for you on Discover and Home (Missing Essentials, the Weekly Exploration playlist and the Discover teaser included), radio shelves, the similar artists a station adds and the albums it plays, playlist suggestions, the similar albums on an album's page, the similar artists on an artist's page, and the albums the Weekly Mix tops up with or requests by itself. Charts, your own listening shown back (such as your Last.fm charts), shelves of your library, search and an artist's own albums are not filtered. To see everything you told Discover, choose What you told Discover at the top of the Discover page. Undo there takes one back, for your account only, and a later queue or page can show it again; the queue you have now is left as it is. Albums hidden before typed answers keep the year they had; an admin can shorten that under Settings > Advanced, in the Retention section. To hide the queue, switch Discover Queue off under Settings > Discover; that changes only your account. The Customise button on the Discover page opens that tab. A built queue survives a restart and counts as stale after a day, after which the page builds a new one. A build that can't reach a service and would deal fewer picks of its own than the queue you have keeps that queue and is tried again after five minutes; an empty queue says whether a service was down or there was nothing to deal.

Discover keeps a record, for your account, of the albums it offered you and what came of them. When your queue is built or your Top Picks are worked out, each album is noted with where it was offered and the reason it was shown with. The page then notes when an album was on screen (counted once a day), when you played its sample and when you opened it from the card. Once a day the server adds, from what it already holds, when you asked for the album and on which page, when it reached the library, when you first played it within 90 days of that and how many plays it had in those first 30 and 90 days, and what you told Discover about it. Requests, discovery batches and what you tell Discover also keep the page they were made on. An album that was never on screen leaves the record 30 days after it was last offered, and one you saw and did nothing with after 13 months; an album you sampled, opened, asked for, played or answered stays. Deleting your account removes all of it. No page shows the record yet, to you or anyone else.

## Your history

What the app keeps about you: your plays here, in the web player and in music apps; a copy of your ListenBrainz history, when you link ListenBrainz or add an export file; the albums Discover offered you and what came of them (see The discover queue above); how each playback ended (see Music apps below); and what you told Discover. Discover also notes which of its pages and which artist pages you opened in the last day, so they stay up to date; that note goes after a day.

When you link ListenBrainz under Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery, the server imports your history by itself, with your own link: it asks ListenBrainz for an export of your listens, waits while ListenBrainz makes it (usually minutes; it asks how it is going after a minute, then less and less often, at most every 15 minutes, and gives up after a day), downloads it and reads it in. The line under Listening history says "Importing your ListenBrainz history" meanwhile, then how many listens are kept and when they were last checked. After that, while you keep using the app, it reads your new listens from ListenBrainz every six hours, at most ten pages of a thousand at a time (past that it asks for a new export of what is left). Linking, or opening your Profile or Discover, starts your import at once. For anyone who has linked ListenBrainz and used the app (a music app counts) in the last seven days without doing any of those, as after an upgrade, the first import starts by itself at a random time within two days, so a server never asks ListenBrainz for everyone's export at once. With `DISCOVER_WARMER_ENABLED=false` only your own visits start the import and the reads. Nothing is read for someone who hasn't used the app for a week, nor while an admin has ListenBrainz reads switched off, and these reads count toward the daily ceiling on Discover's background reads (see the kept facts above). If a read fails, as when a token is revoked, the next waits six hours, and longer while they keep failing, up to a week. Disconnecting stops the reads and keeps what was imported. If ListenBrainz cannot make the export, the line says so and Import again asks for another. Linking a different ListenBrainz account than the one your history is kept from asks first: on yes, the history kept from the old account is replaced by the new one's. A history you added from a file becomes your linked account's with its first read, so a later link to another account replaces the file's listens too.

To add your ListenBrainz history from a file instead, download an export of your listens from ListenBrainz (a ZIP file) and choose Add a ListenBrainz export file under Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery. The file can be up to 512 MB. The server reads it in the background, keeps each listen once, leaves out the listens that are your plays here coming back from ListenBrainz, and deletes the file once it is read; the line beside the button says how many listens it holds. Adding the same file again, or a newer export, adds only what is new. An imported listen is never a play here: it changes no play count, scrobble or statistic.

Discover works from a picture of your taste that the server keeps and rebuilds by itself: when you visit and it is due, and in the background about once a day while you use the app, a few hours after you play something, and soon after your ListenBrainz history is added. It weighs every artist you played here or in your ListenBrainz history, with the hearts, ratings, follows, playlists, requests and links that make seed artists (see Discover above), and groups your seed artists by the similar artists the server keeps, with no other service asked. Each group is named after its heaviest seed artist, as "Around" that artist. Every artist your own plays, marks and links name counts as known to you, and so does every album you played here. While a similar-artists list a group needs is still being fetched, the groups you had stay as they were and the rest is brought up to date. While your ListenBrainz history here is up to date, your seed artists and the albums you played are no longer read from ListenBrainz, and Your Top Albums counts your plays here once they cover at least three albums in the range shown.

To forget it, choose Forget my history under Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery and confirm. It removes the record of the albums Discover offered you, the copy of your ListenBrainz history, how your plays ended, the note of the pages you opened, the picture of your taste worked out from them, and your saved Discover page, home shelves and discover queue, which are built again. Your plays and play counts, your library, your requests and discovery batches (with the page each was made on) and what you told Discover stay. If something stops it part way, choosing Forget my history again finishes the rest. Nothing is imported from ListenBrainz again until you add a file or choose Import again. The record starts again with the next albums Discover offers you. A backup made earlier still holds what it held. Deleting your account removes all of it.

## Radio

Radio plays a station from an artist, an album, a genre or a whole shelf. An artist station uses that artist and up to seven similar ones; a genre station starts from artists in your library tagged with that genre (the same ones all day) and tops up with Last.fm's top artists for the tag. In library mode it plays only your own tracks. In hybrid mode, the default, about half the tracks are popular songs by those artists that you don't own. Songs you don't own play through YouTube when it is set up and are skipped otherwise. No artist gets more than four tracks, and never three in a row. Playing a shelf plays its albums in order: your own tracks where you have the album, and its first few songs where you don't. Radio shelves of albums work the same way: similar artists' albums for an artist, similar albums and more by the same artist for an album. They follow ListenBrainz, or Last.fm while ListenBrainz is having trouble, and need one of the two linked for your account.

## Playlist suggestions

"Suggest Albums" on a playlist's page lists albums to add: albums by artists similar to the ones on it, plus albums in their genres. Artists already on the playlist are left out. It works for your own playlists and public ones. Suggestions start from the artists' MusicBrainz ids. A playlist imported from Jellyfin, Navidrome or Plex doesn't carry those, so each track's artist is looked up in your library: by the track's MusicBrainz recording id, or else by the artist's name when exactly one library artist linked to MusicBrainz has that name. Tracks whose artist isn't found that way don't shape the suggestions. When there is nothing to suggest, the window says why.

## Download all

"Download all" on a Discover shelf creates a discovery batch: every album on it that you don't have and nobody has asked for yet becomes a normal album request. Your quota and the approval rules apply as they would for one album, so a plain user's batch waits for an admin, and a batch that would go over your quota is refused as a whole. Albums you already own or that are already requested are listed as skipped. A batch holds up to 30 albums. Your batches are listed with how many albums have arrived and how many are still pending. Removing a batch withdraws your pending requests from it; if someone else asked for the same album, their request carries on. When an admin removes a batch, the albums it brought in also go to the recycle bin, unless another user asked for, favorited or played them. It never touches albums that were already yours or already requested. If a step fails, that album stays in the batch so you can try again. While no recycle bin is known (its folder can't be reached, say), albums stay in the batch the same way and the page says why. You can also remove just the batch and keep the albums. Pages can send an `idempotency_key` (a UUID) when creating a batch, so a retried "Download all" never files it twice.

## Weekly Mix

Your Weekly Mix is a playlist built for each user with a linked ListenBrainz account, while ListenBrainz recommendations are on. It takes your ListenBrainz weekly-jams and weekly-exploration playlists and tops them up to 100 tracks with the most played song of artists similar to the ones in them, read with your own ListenBrainz link. Without a link there is no Weekly Mix. Songs you already own play from your library. The server rebuilds every mix once a day if it is more than six days old, and you can rebuild yours any time with Refresh under Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery. If you switch on "auto-request" there, each build also requests up to five albums from the mix you don't have yet. Admins and trusted users get this straight away; other users wait for an admin to approve it on the Requests page. An admin can reject or later revoke it, which also switches the toggle off. The app rebuilds your Weekly Mix by itself, and importing a Spotify playlist again replaces its songs, so changes that people you let edit these playlists make are replaced at the next rebuild or import.

## Covers and artist pictures

Album covers come from your own files first: an image in the album folder (`cover.jpg`, `folder.png`, `front.jpg` and the like) or a picture embedded in a track. An image in a folder that holds tracks of albums with more than one title is not used for any of them, so each takes the picture embedded in its own files, if it has one. The library picks these up after each scan, and large pictures are scaled down for the grid. Albums without local art get their cover from the Cover Art Archive, cached on disk. To prefer the Cover Art Archive over your files, turn off "Prefer local cover art" under Settings > Advanced.

Artist pictures come from TheAudioDB. When TheAudioDB has none, DroppedNeedle finds the artist's Wikidata entry by its MusicBrainz id and uses the Deezer picture that entry links to, or else its Wikimedia Commons photo. The match is always by exact id, never by name. A Commons photo shows its author and license in a small line on the artist page, linked to the file's Commons page. The first time an artist shows up the picture is fetched in the background, so a grid of new artists fills in over a few seconds instead of holding the page up. Pictures are kept on disk with the covers. AudioDB album thumbnails stand in for albums that have no cover anywhere else. A library artist that is not linked to MusicBrainz shows a mosaic of their own albums' covers (art in the album's files): four of them in a 2x2, two crosswise when there are only two or three, or the one cover when that is all there is. Albums under their name come first, then albums not tagged as compilations, then the newest. Subsonic and Jellyfin apps show the same picture.

A song in a playlist shows its picture only when it comes from DroppedNeedle itself or from Spotify, Deezer, Last.fm, TheAudioDB or YouTube; any other picture address is dropped and the song shows the placeholder, so nobody can add a picture that tracks who views a public or shared playlist.

When the Cover Art Archive or another picture source cannot be reached, the cover keeps loading rather than showing as missing, and it is asked for again about 30 seconds later. Only an answer from the source that it has no art shows the placeholder.

The server also fetches everything for your library ahead of time: two minutes after a library scan finishes, a library image refresh starts on its own (unless one is already running). An admin can start one any time with "Refresh library images" under Settings > Cache. It goes through your artists (page details and pictures), their top tracks, your albums (details and covers) and finally anything TheAudioDB has not been asked about yet. Their similar artists it leaves to the kept facts' background job. It skips what is already cached, so running it again picks up where the last run stopped. A progress pill at the bottom of the page shows where it is; admins and trusted users can stop it from there. Settings > Cache shows the cached provider answers per source, the kept facts, the cached images and the database size, and clears the provider data or the images when you want a fresh start. Clearing the provider answers also drops the kept facts and everyone's built Discover page, home shelves and discover queue; each is built again on its next visit.

## Last.fm and ListenBrainz

Last.fm works like this: the admin registers one Last.fm API application at last.fm/api/account/create, saves its key and shared secret under Settings > Last.fm and switches Last.fm on, once for the whole server. From 1 December 2026 Last.fm accepts a key only while the account that owns it has Last.fm Pro: the admin's account for the server's key, the user's for their own. Without Pro, Last.fm refuses the key and its features stop for everyone using it. "Test connection" there checks the pair before or after saving it and says which one Last.fm turned down: an invalid key, a suspended key, or a shared secret that does not match the key. Each user then clicks Connect under Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery, approves DroppedNeedle on last.fm, and clicks Finish. Their scrobbles go to their own Last.fm account. Users can also bring their own API application: if the server has no key saved, Connect asks for the user's own key and secret first, and a user can always store a pair through the API (`PUT /api/v3/me/connections/lastfm`). A user's own pair replaces the server's key for that user only. The Last.fm sections on artist and album pages follow the same rule: they read with the user's own key when one is saved, and with the server's key otherwise. Last.fm charts on the home page read with your own key when you saved one, and the server's key otherwise; "your top albums" from Last.fm read your linked account. If the admin later changes the server's key, everyone who linked with the old one has to click Connect again: Last.fm sessions belong to the key that created them. Link ListenBrainz with the token from your ListenBrainz profile. Scrobbles are saved with the play: if Last.fm or ListenBrainz is down or slow, or the server restarts, they are sent later, as long as the play is less than two weeks old. Turning forwarding off, or unlinking, stops the ones still waiting, and so does a service refusing your token; link again to scrobble new plays. When the admin switches Last.fm off, the Last.fm scrobbles still waiting are dropped, and Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery says "Last.fm is off on this server." instead of offering Connect, with Last.fm scrobbling off and Home and Discover following ListenBrainz and no longer offering a Connect Last.fm card (the API answers `409 LASTFM_OFF`; a missing key pair answers `409 PROVIDER_NOT_CONFIGURED`). Artist pages show photos from AudioDB, Deezer and Wikimedia Commons (AudioDB is on by default, free key rate limits apply), with the AudioDB key and whether your browser loads its images directly or through DroppedNeedle under Settings > Advanced. Artist thumbnails across the app use the same pictures.

An artist page's popular songs and popular albums come from the listening service you chose, and the albums in an album page's "You Might Also Like" from your primary source. Similar artists, on both pages, are the one list described under Discover, whichever service you chose. When a service is switched off, not set up, has nothing or cannot be reached, the other one fills in, either way round, and on an artist page a line above the lists says so.

ListenBrainz recommendations are on unless an admin turns them off under Settings > ListenBrainz, and they need no account. Album and artist pages, Discover and the listen counts shown on search results read ListenBrainz with your own ListenBrainz link when you have one, and without an account otherwise. Library Management's genres always go out without one, and so do the counts search sorts by: with ListenBrainz as your primary source, well-matched albums and songs are ordered by how often people play them, using counts already fetched, and missing ones are fetched in the background for your next search (about two ListenBrainz calls for a new search, kept 30 days), so search never waits on ListenBrainz. Last.fm followers' searches ask ListenBrainz nothing, though their similar artists still come from ListenBrainz. ListenBrainz shows an artist's popular albums and songs only to a linked account, so without your own link the server does not ask it for the artist page's lists. They then come from Last.fm when you have it, and from what is already fetched for other people. Otherwise an artist page says to link ListenBrainz under Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery. The albums Discover, the queue, radio and album pages offer from an artist need no link, and nobody's link is used for them. That is not an outage, so no "unavailable" warning shows for it. The server never reads with an account of its own. With the switch off, nothing in the app reads ListenBrainz: Discover, Home, the queue, radio, Your Weekly Mix and Wrapped included. Shelves then come from Last.fm where it is set up, and say "Recommendations are turned off." where nothing else can answer. Linking your own ListenBrainz account and scrobbling to it work either way.

A popular song that names no album, as Last.fm's don't, is looked up on MusicBrainz in the background the first time the artist's page opens (at most 20 lookups a page, kept for a week), so it shows its album's cover from the next visit. A popular song you have on one of the artist's own albums, or on the album it is shown with, shows that album and plays your copy.

## Music apps

Music apps (Subsonic and Jellyfin) sign in with an app password you make under Profile > Connect Apps, or, for apps an admin has listed, through the browser ([Signing in apps from the browser](app-connect.md)). Your apps list your own playlists and those shared with you, and you can add, remove and reorder songs in a shared one when its owner lets you edit it. The switch under Profile > Connect Apps, off by default for everyone, adds other people's public playlists to those lists. It changes only what apps list: an app that already holds a public playlist's id can still open it with the switch off, a private playlist never opens for anyone it isn't shared with, and the web UI is unaffected. What you play in an app counts like a play in the web player: it shows in the app's recently played and most played lists, with play counts and last-played times. A play in the web player counts, whether or not you scrobble, once you have heard half the track or four minutes of it (tracks under 30 seconds never count). A play through a connected server, a radio YouTube pick, a YouTube track link or an imported playlist entry counts as your library's track when it carries that track's MusicBrainz recording id. A YouTube link takes that id from the album's tracklist, so it says which track the link stands for, not what the video holds. A full-album video carries none, and neither does a playlist entry imported by an older version or moved to another source. The id also goes to ListenBrainz with these plays when you forward them. Playing a Jellyfin, Navidrome or Plex track in the web player also reports it to that server under your own linked account (Profile > Media Server Accounts), so it shows there as it would from that server's own apps: in its Now Playing, play counts and recently played. Without a linked account of your own nothing is reported there. Navidrome counts the play once you have heard half the track or four minutes of it, and passes it on to Last.fm or ListenBrainz when you linked them inside Navidrome. Leave "Let Navidrome handle Last.fm and ListenBrainz" on under Profile > Scrobbling & Discovery in that case; with it off, such a play is scrobbled twice. Your DroppedNeedle history holds one play per listen either way. Previews never count. The server also keeps, for you, how each playback ended: what played, how long you heard it (for a music app, the last position it reported), whether it finished or was skipped, stopped or failed, and what it was started from, such as an album, a playlist or a station. This record is apart from your plays and never changes a play count, a scrobble or a statistic. Marking a song, album or artist played or unplayed in a Jellyfin app is kept for you alone. Marking an album marks each of its songs, marking an artist marks the songs on the albums they lead, and an album counts as played once all its songs are. Unplayed hides your earlier plays from your Jellyfin apps' played mark and play count, and leaves your history, statistics, Discover and Subsonic apps as they were. A Subsonic app needs to report only when playback starts, pauses, resumes or seeks: you show as playing until the track would end, plus 45 seconds, and a pause for 45 seconds. Last.fm and ListenBrainz hear that a track is playing once per track, again only when it repeats or after a stop, and never for a pause. Jellyfin apps report every few seconds and drop out 45 seconds after their last report. Subsonic apps get each album's and artist's MusicBrainz ids, and Jellyfin apps each album's and track's, from DroppedNeedle's match, or, before identification has reached the album, the ids the files' tags agree on (an album whose match was taken off, or that identification found nothing for, sends none); tag ids are passed to apps only and never become an album's or artist's identity. A track is only transcoded when the app asks for another format or a lower bitrate. If the app names no bitrate, Opus streams at 128 kbps and MP3 at 192 kbps, never above the max bitrate you set. A bitrate asked for below 64 kbps streams at 64 kbps. Each user can run two transcodes at once, so gapless players can load the next track early. A transcode an app has not read for 30 minutes is stopped to free its slot, and the track may need playing again. Finamp's transcoded playback uses HLS, which needs transcoding on and ffmpeg installed. Only sign-in attempts are rate limited per IP address. A run of wrong passwords from one address locks it out for a short while. A device repeating one wrong password counts once a minute and is held to about five tries a second, and an app still using a revoked app password does not count at all, so neither can lock out other apps on the same network. A Subsonic app that signs in with a token and salt counts every try, as each try looks new. Album and artist pictures in Subsonic apps come from signed links under `/subsonic/img` that open without signing in, so an app's picture loader can show them; playlist covers and music always need sign-in. A link is the same for every user and does not expire. It stops working when the album or artist leaves the library, while the Subsonic API is off, or if the server's data key is replaced, and the app gets a new link the next time it reads the album or artist. A changed album cover reaches apps the next time they read the album, and Finamp within a day. A changed artist picture can take longer to reach apps. Jellyfin apps load album and artist pictures without signing in too, as they would from a real Jellyfin server, but only by ids that this server alone can make: album and artist ids are made with the server's data key, so nobody without an account can work one out or test whether an album is in your library. Song pictures come from the song's album. Every id handed out is stored, so apps keep their pins and downloads across restarts. A stored album or artist id survives a replaced `config/data_enc.key` (`droppedneedle-tool reset-key`); only items added afterwards get ids under the new key. Whoever holds an old key can still work out the picture ids of albums and artists stored before it was replaced.

## Spotify playlist import

Spotify playlist import needs an app from the Spotify developer dashboard. Add the redirect URI that Settings > Spotify shows (it ends in `/api/v3/acquire/spotify/auth/callback`) to the app, then save the app's client ID and secret there. With "Redirect Origin" left empty, that address is the one DroppedNeedle works out for itself, which is right when you reach it directly or through a reverse proxy listed in `TRUSTED_PROXY_IPS` that sends `X-Forwarded-Proto`; if it is not the address in your browser's address bar, fill in Redirect Origin. Each user then clicks Connect on the Spotify card in their profile, and Disconnect there unlinks it again. An app set up for v2 lists v2's address, which v3 does not answer: add the v3 one in the dashboard (see [Upgrading from v2](upgrading-from-v2.md#before-you-upgrade)).

An import runs in the background, one at a time across all users, and picks up again after a restart. Each album on the playlist is matched to its MusicBrainz release group only when MusicBrainz lists one of the album's track ISRCs on a release under the album's title. A multi-track release MusicBrainz lists no ISRCs for at all counts instead when its title, one of its artists, its track count (at least two tracks) and its type are the album's, and every song of the album on the playlist is on it; one-track singles still need an ISRC match, and a release that lists other ISRCs never counts that way. An album that can't be proved either way is left unmatched rather than guessed by name. The ISRCs are looked up first, so every edition counts and a soundtrack or compilation is found as well as an artist's album. When the same recording is on an album and a single of the same name, Spotify's album type and then its track count say which one it is.

Once an import finishes, each song whose matched album is in your library plays from the library's copy: the track at the same disc and track number when its title agrees, else the track with the same title elsewhere on that album, else the track at that place when the titles differ only by a "(feat. ...)" credit, "&" against "and", "/" spacing or apostrophes. Songs on an album that comes in later link the same way a few seconds after it lands, however it arrives (a download, the import folder, a scan, or an identification that names the album later), with no need to open the playlist. Those songs then also show in Subsonic and Jellyfin apps, which list only songs with a library file.

## Playlist files

A playlist's menu exports it as M3U (extended M3U8) or CSV, and "Import playlist" on the Playlists page reads one of those files back into a new private playlist of yours, named after the file.

An export lists each entry's file by its path inside its library folder, such as `Portishead/Dummy/01 Mysterons.flac`. If your player keeps music under a folder of its own, add a path prefix (`Music/`, say) and it goes before every path; the export window remembers it in this browser. Entries with no file in your library (a Spotify or YouTube entry, a removed track) are left out, and the export says how many. A CSV has a header row and the columns `title`, `artist`, `album`, `album artist`, `duration` (whole seconds) and `path`. A cell that starts with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a carriage return gets a `'` in front, so a spreadsheet shows it as text; the import takes that `'` off again.

An import takes `.m3u`, `.m3u8` and `.csv` files of up to 2 MB. A CSV may have its columns in any order and other columns besides, as long as its header names at least one of the columns above; without such a header the export's column order is assumed. The import also reads the column names common Spotify exporters write (`Track Name`, `Artist Name(s)`, `Album Name` and the like). A file that starts with a byte-order mark is read the same as one without. Each entry is matched to a track in your library, in this order:

1. its path, exactly as the library holds it;
2. the end of its path, from a folder boundary, so a prefix such as `Music/` or `D:\Music\` doesn't matter;
3. its title, artist and album, ignoring capitals, accents and spacing;
4. its title and artist the same way;
5. if none of those finds it and its artist cell names several artists, such as `A, B` or `A; B`, its title, the first of them and its album, the same way.

Only tracks in your library are added, each once, in the file's order. The import reports how many tracks it added and each entry it left out, with its title, artist and why (the page lists the first 200; the API answer holds them all): it names no file, nothing in your library matches it, or the same track came earlier in the file. It reads the first 10,000 entries; any past that are only counted, and the report says how many.

## Song links

Paste a song link from Deezer, Apple Music, Spotify or YouTube into search, a Deezer short link (`link.deezer.com`), or a song.link address made from a Deezer, Apple Music or Spotify link, and search shows that song: it plays when your library has it, and you can request it when it doesn't. The clipboard button at the end of the search box pastes and searches in one tap, and on Android, once DroppedNeedle is installed as an app, Share > DroppedNeedle in another app opens the search with the link.

A song link searched from a Subsonic app that searches on the server finds your library's copy of that song in the app's song results; a song you don't have yet shows nothing there.

Deezer and Spotify links are matched by the song's ISRC, and every MusicBrainz recording filed under that ISRC is shown. Apple Music links give no ISRC, so the song is matched by title and artist, and only a close match is shown: a high MusicBrainz score and a length within three seconds of the store's. A YouTube link is matched by its video's title, read as "Artist - Title" with tags such as "(Official Video)" dropped, or by the channel for an auto-generated "Artist - Topic" video. YouTube gives no length, so a remix or live video can miss. A Deezer short link is followed one step, and works when it leads to a song. When nothing matches, search says so rather than guess.

Spotify links need Spotify set up by an admin under Settings > Spotify: they are read with that app, without anyone's sign-in. While Spotify is not set up or is switched off, search says so. YouTube links work while an admin has YouTube switched on.

Each new link pasted costs one call to its service (two for a Deezer short link) and one MusicBrainz search. Spotify also hands out a token, which is reused until it expires, about an hour. A YouTube link is read through YouTube's embed information, which spends none of the daily YouTube quota. Both answers are kept for a day, so pasting the same link again asks nothing.

On an iPhone, a shortcut puts DroppedNeedle in the share sheet. In the Shortcuts app, make a shortcut, turn on Show in Share Sheet and let it receive URLs and text. Add three actions: URL Encode with the Shortcut Input, a Text action holding your server's address, then `/search?q=`, then the encoded text, and Open URLs with that text.
