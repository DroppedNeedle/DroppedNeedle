# Your library

How DroppedNeedle scans your music, works out which album and edition each folder is, and lets you fix its grouping. Scanning and identification never rename or retag a file. Writing tags and filing music is [Library Management](library-management.md).

## Browsing

The Albums, Artists and Tracks lists show the first page and add the next with Load more, however large the library. Albums sort newest first, by title or by artist; the list keeps the order you last chose in this browser, and signing out forgets it. Jump to... scrolls to the first album under a letter, loading more as it goes: by title, or by artist on Artist A-Z. From Newest First or Recently Favourited it switches to Title A-Z. Accents don't count, so "Élan" is under E. A jump stops at 2,000 albums. Tracks sort by date added, title, artist (then album and track number) or album. Shuffle on the Tracks page plays every track once in a random order.

An artist's page also has an "Appears on" section when they are credited on albums someone else leads, such as a guest or featured spot. It shows those albums and tracks, whether or not the artist has albums of their own, and the Contributors view of the Artists list counts them: an artist with albums of their own is listed there too. When an album has an identification review waiting, curators see "Needs you: open its review" under its edition plate.

An artist's page lists every album of theirs you have under "In your library", split into Albums, EPs & Singles, and Compilations, Live & more by its MusicBrainz type, including the kinds your release-type settings leave out of the discography below it. Without MusicBrainz an album is filed by its compilation flag. An artist MusicBrainz credits on releases but on no release group of its own, such as a band whose album is credited to its singer, gets the albums of those releases as its discography, the way MusicBrainz's own artist page lists them.

Album cards say how many tracks you have, and "3/12 tracks" while some of the edition's tracks are missing. On an artist's page a discography row of an album you have says the same; copies of one album add up, to the edition's count at most. The edition's count is the track count of the MusicBrainz edition the album is matched to, all discs together, once the server holds that edition's tracklist (an album page visit or the library precache fetches it; listing albums never asks MusicBrainz). Otherwise, and for a guessed match your files' tags contradict, it is the track totals in the files' tags, added up over the discs, and only when every disc has one. When neither tells, the card shows only the tracks you have. Subsonic apps get the same count as `releaseSongCount`, beside `songCount`, and it is left out when unknown. Files scanned by an older version have their track totals read on the next scan.

The Albums, Artists and Tracks lists have a Filter button, and each of its filters works either way round. On Albums and Artists it sets a number of tracks you have, the number each card shows (on Artists, an album artist's tracks on the albums they lead, a contributor's appearances): At least that many, or Fewer than. On Albums and Tracks it shows only the formats you mark, or with Without hides them; an album's format is the one most of its tracks have. Your rating keeps what you gave at least, or at most, so many stars; what you haven't rated is left out. The button shows how many filters are on. The filters stay in the page's address, and each list keeps yours in the browser: coming back to it without a filter in the link brings them back, and signing out forgets them.

## Scanning

Scans live on the Scanning tab of Library Management. A running scan can be paused, resumed or stopped there; stopping keeps everything it already read. New music shows up in the library as the scan finds it, not only when the scan ends. Rescan files rereads every file in the folders you pick, even ones that look unchanged, which helps after you fix files with another tool. Each finished run in Recent runs lists the files it could not read or skipped, each with a short reason and what to do about it (for example, a damaged file says to re-download or replace it). If someone changed the library folders since you opened the page, the scan is refused: reload and try again.

While the library is switched off under Settings > Library, nothing changes your files: scans, Library Management, removals to the recycle bin, Put back, Empty recycle bin, imports of held files and plugin writes are refused, the recycle bin's expiry waits, and downloads wait (the Downloads page says so) and start and come into the library by themselves once it is back on.

Between scheduled scans, DroppedNeedle checks your library folders every few minutes and starts a scan when files change. Turn this off with "Watch folders for changes" under Settings > Library > Automatic scanning; scheduled and manual scans still run.

A library folder that is away, such as a share that is not mounted, loses nothing: its albums' covers and lyrics come back with it.

Settings > Library won't let you remove your last library path while the library still has tracks in it. Set the path to Excluded instead if you want it left alone. If a path disappears from the settings while its tracks are still in the library, the page offers to put it back.

Sub-folder rules match folder names without regard to letter case or Unicode form, on every disk. On a case-sensitive disk a rule `Live` also covers a separate `live` folder: a scan treats `live/` as Excluded when the rule is Excluded, and when the rule is included under an excluded folder it walks both. If such a folder is renamed so only its capitals change, a track whose file is at the same place under the new name goes missing at the old one, and its plays, favorites and ratings move across when the match is clear. Tracks it can't tell apart stay missing at the old name with their history.

A scan never follows a link. A folder replaced by a link is not read and its tracks stay as they are, on every scan and whichever folders it covers; Recent runs lists it. Put a real folder back and the next scan reads it again.

After a scan, the library notes which tracks carry lyrics (a lyrics tag, or a `.lrc` file named like the track); it only reads them.

Recent runs can also export a diagnostic report for a scan: a small JSON file with the run's counters, timings, scopes and failed files, to attach to a bug report. Paths are replaced by fingerprints, and it holds no user names, passwords or error text.

MusicBrainz lookups pace at 1 request per second on the official MusicBrainz server. Built-in BrainzMash runs its own local pacing instead. Either way, later scans are incremental, so the first one is the slow one.

## Supported formats

The library takes FLAC, MP3, Ogg Vorbis, Opus, AAC, ALAC or AAC in M4A, and WAV. Scans and imports skip other formats, WMA, APE and WavPack included.

When your browser can't play a track, the web player asks the server for a transcode by itself, for library files and for Jellyfin, Navidrome and Plex tracks alike: FLAC for a lossless source such as ALAC, so nothing is lost (the max bitrate under Settings > Connect Apps doesn't apply to it), and Opus or MP3 for a lossy one. Seeking works as usual, unless the track's length isn't known: then the seek bar is greyed out. The player bar shows the track's own format, with "Converted" beside it while a transcode plays. A media server track is copied to a temporary file on the server while it is transcoded, and the file is removed when playback ends. Navidrome names an M4A file without saying whether it holds AAC or ALAC, and the player doesn't know a Plex track's format, so such a track is tried as it is first and transcoded when it doesn't play. When the server itself won't send a track (your media server isn't answering, the file isn't there, or the server is busy), the player says so and doesn't try a transcode.

The "On-the-fly transcoding" switch under Settings > Connect Apps turns these transcodes on and off, the same switch music apps use, and they need ffmpeg (the Docker image has it). With the switch off, or without ffmpeg, a track your browser can't play is skipped with one message saying why, and nothing is downloaded for it. The queue marks it "Can't play", and so does any later track the player already knows it can't play. An admin's message has a link to turn transcoding on. A transcode that fails is skipped the same way. Album formats, the format filter and saved playlist entries name ALAC apart from AAC; the per-format track counts on the library's statistics still count both as M4A.

## How an album is identified

When most of its files carry a MusicBrainz release ID (Picard and beets write one), DroppedNeedle looks that release up and checks every track against it. Otherwise it searches by album and artist and fetches the best few releases. When nothing turns up, it searches again: without edition and disc words ("Deluxe", "CD1"), then on the title's words in any order, then by the first track's title and the artist. An album filed under "Unknown Artist" is searched under the artist most of its tracks name. Once it knows which album it is, it also lists every edition of that album and checks the ones that could hold your files, so the right pressing is weighed even when search missed it. Each file is paired with a track by title, track number and length. A track MusicBrainz titles as a piece and its movements ("Close to the Edge: I. The Solid Time of Change / ...") also pairs with a file titled with the piece alone, and artists joined differently ("A; B" and "A & B") count as the same credit. An album may hold one file the release doesn't list (two from 21 files on), or, when every track of the release has its file, one for every four paired files; more sends the album to review. When a file's MusicBrainz release ID and recording ID both point to the release but its track ID belongs to another edition (common in downloaded files), the track ID is treated as a tagging mistake, as Picard does: the file is matched by its recording, the edition screen and the file's row on the album page say so, and the right ID is written when the edition's tags are, by writing the edition's tags from the album page or by a Library Management profile that replaces MusicBrainz IDs (both presets do; one that keeps existing values leaves the old ID).

If MusicBrainz has merged a recording since your files were tagged, a file's old recording ID counts as the recording it was merged into. DroppedNeedle asks MusicBrainz about every recording ID none of the candidate releases carries, up to 100 per album. It keeps an answer for a week, and a "no such recording" answer for 10 minutes. A recording ID tag that is not a MusicBrainz ID is not looked up and counts against every release. Downloads being imported are checked the same way.

When every file's tags name the same MusicBrainz release group, or the album is matched to a release group without an edition yet, the album is matched with that group in mind. If the files also name a release that has since moved to another group, the release's group counts. Without a release to look up, that group's editions are checked first; a group MusicBrainz merged counts as the group it was merged into, and one it no longer has is ignored. A release of that group wins a close call with a release of another group, such as one file of an album against the single of the same name. A release of another group that fits clearly better is only applied as a best guess, marked unconfirmed, and it stays unconfirmed until you confirm it or the files' tags name its group. A close call won this way can be undone like any automatic edition ([Library Management](library-management.md#undoing-an-automatic-edition)), and so can a guess that replaced a match with no edition.

Between editions that fit equally well it looks at the barcode, catalog number, media, country and disc count your tags carry (a catalog number of "[none]", as Picard writes for a release without one, counts as none), then at how closely the exact track lengths agree (a remaster or a digital release is often a second or two off the CD), then at your edition preferences.

An album matched by itself moves to another edition of the same album when it is identified again and the barcode or catalog number on more than half its files names that edition, while the edition it has states a different one. The new edition must fit your files as well as any confirmed match must, and must not contradict more of their media, country and disc count than the old one. A MusicBrainz release ID in the files wins: if any file carries a release ID that may be of that album, it keeps its edition. Media or country alone never move an album, and an edition someone chose, a custom edition or one carried over from v2 as chosen never moves. The album page shows the new edition and lists the move under "Album history", and an admin can undo the move like any automatic edition ([Library Management](library-management.md#undoing-an-automatic-edition)); an album whose automatic edition was undone is not moved again. Under Library Management, the new edition's tags may be written to the files, and a folder named from the release date may be renamed after the reissue's date. After the upgrade, each album matched by itself this way is checked once, in the background, once its files' edition tags have been read.

Fingerprinting stays off until you add an AcoustID API key (Settings > Library). The key lets DroppedNeedle identify untagged music by its audio and check downloads are the songs you asked for. With a key, only albums whose tags don't match well get fingerprinted, twenty files per AcoustID request. A fingerprint helps a track match when AcoustID hears that track's recording. It counts against a match only when what AcoustID heard is another candidate's track; a recording none of the candidates has says nothing, since AcoustID links many reissues and remasters to an older recording. Audio alone never moves a file off a track whose title clearly matches it when the track number or length fits too. A title still clearly matches with an addition in brackets or a "feat." credit ("Song (Remaster)", "Song (LP Version)", "Song (feat. X)" match "Song"), unless another track of the release matches it better, such as "Song (Remix)". The album is then left as a best guess for you to check, with a note that the audio and the titles disagree. One case settles itself: when the file's length, measured from its audio, is within 3 seconds of its own track and more than 3 seconds off every track AcoustID heard, the fingerprint is set aside and the album can be matched as if the file had none. The candidate's track evidence shows `acoustid_overruled` for that file. An MP3 without a Xing, Info or VBRI header has only an estimated length, which never settles a dispute. An album that wants its audio heard waits while AcoustID can't be reached, and is identified by itself once it answers.

An edition someone chose is never changed by identification, a rescan or a move. Editions carried over from v2 count as chosen: the custom editions and the pinned editions v2 had. The one exception is the cleanup's fold of an album v2 split in two (below), which drops the v2 match of the half it folds and can be undone from the album's history. Matches identification made, and those v2 took from the files' tags, stay open: a later pass may replace them, and new evidence that contradicts one takes it off, which "Album history" on the album page lists. Undo on the album page puts a match taken off this way back; it may be taken off again if the files change. If an album's files change while it is being identified, nothing is applied and the album is identified again on its new files.

Identification has its own Pause button on the Scanning tab of Library Management. The album being identified finishes, nothing new starts until you press Resume, and new scans keep queueing albums in the meantime.

## Check albums

DroppedNeedle always picks. A clear match is applied. Files that make up a whole release, every track a close fit and the album title and artist agreeing, are a clear match even without MusicBrainz IDs in their tags, so a one-track single confirms on its own. When the files fit two releases about equally well, such as a single and the album of the same name, the one they make up whole wins. A file only completes a release when its song title matches in full, so "Sunrise" never confirms as "Sunrise (Instrumental)". When the files make up two releases equally well and one is an official release while the other exists only as a promotional copy, the official one wins. Unless every file carries its MusicBrainz track ID, a live album, or a compilation whose tags don't say it is one, confirms only when no other release group comes close and the files fit it almost exactly: every track there, every song title matching in full, every length measured and within 3 seconds. If another release of the same live album could be a different night's recording of those songs, the album only confirms when a measured length more than 3 seconds off rules that other release out. A close call, a loose match, or a live or compilation release that fits less closely gets the best guess applied too, marked "unconfirmed": pages, playback, other apps and downloads use it, but tagging and organizing leave its files alone until you confirm it with "That's right" (or turn on "Also manage unconfirmed matches" in Settings > Editions). A later scan can confirm it on its own, and so can a download of that same edition that lands as the album rather than only filling in its missing tracks; a best guess whose files' tags name another album stays unconfirmed. When MusicBrainz can't give a readable copy of the release your files name, the album gets its best-guess edition, marked unconfirmed; when a release it couldn't read leaves nothing that fits, the album waits and is tried again later instead of being marked "no match". Only when every candidate is ruled out does the album keep its own tags, marked "no match", with the closest candidates one click away.

Check albums (in the menu for curators, with a quiet count) lists these albums under Best guess and Not found, each tab with its count; Home and the library overview count them too. Nothing waits on you: "We're not sure which edition these are. They still play. Files stay untouched until you say." Each row shows your files (cover, title, artist, how many files) beside the guess "That's right" would confirm (its cover, title, artist and how many of your files it fits), and why it is a guess, in words such as "Your files say Dummy. This fits Dummy (Deluxe Edition) better." when the files' tags name another album, "Two albums fit about equally. We picked this one.", "Closest we found, but some tracks differ." or "2 tracks sound like other songs.", with the sheet naming those tracks "That's right" confirms the guess and shows Undo for a few seconds; a guess someone changed meanwhile is refused and the list reloads. An album whose edition a curator took with "Use anyway" shows under Not found as "Needs matching", with "Match files" to match its files by hand. Compare opens the album's edition sheet; its closing button, "Back to Check albums", returns to the list at the next album. Every album page shows the reason under its edition plate to everyone.

Users who aren't curators can press "Looks wrong" under an album's edition plate when its match seems off; pressing it again takes your mark back. Curators see those albums under Looks wrong, with how many people marked each. Choosing, confirming or handing back the album's edition, approving one of its reviews or matching a file by hand clears the marks, and "Looks fine" clears them without a change.

Whether you have an album is answered the same way everywhere: album pages, Discover, Weekly Mix, wanted albums, follows, search and downloads. An album counts for the release group it is matched to, unless it is a best guess whose files' tags name another album. An album with no match yet, or one holding such a guess, counts for the release group all its files' tags name, as long as no file names a different one. An album whose match was taken off, undone or withdrawn, or that identification found nothing for, counts for nothing until it is matched again: its tags don't bring it back. A download never clears a match you took off, or a best guess whose files name another album.

A few cases do wait on a person: an undone automatic edition with nothing to go back to, or editions dropped when two albums were combined. Those sit under Check albums > Needs you; the album page links to its review. For each one you can use one of the listed editions, keep your tags ("Keep my tags": the album keeps any match it has and its own tags), take its MusicBrainz match off ("Remove match"), leave it for later ("Decide later"), look again (start identification again; refused for an album in a managed folder set to keep its own tags, whose edition you choose on its page instead), or (admins) take the album out of the library and put it back later. Keep my tags and Decide later show Reopen for a few seconds after; it works while the album is unchanged and has no other open review. Nothing is deleted from disk. If someone else acted on the review after you opened it, your decision is refused and you're asked to reload the list. Starting identification again settles the album the way a scan does: a clear match is applied, otherwise the best guess, marked unconfirmed. If someone chose the album's edition, it keeps that edition and Look again isn't offered; change the edition on the album page instead. A retry is also refused (`LOCAL_METADATA_MANAGED`) for an album in a folder set to keep its own tags when Library Management is on for that folder.

The list has five views, each with its count: Open, Tags kept, Excluded, Resolved (an edition was used, or the review was left for later) and All. Sort it by newest, oldest, title, artist, folder or most tracks, or group it by reason; each reason's group can keep the tags of all its albums or look again at them all, with the same preview as below. On a phone the filters sit behind "Filters". The view, filters and order are kept in the page address, so a link or a reload opens the same list. After you act on a review, focus moves to the next one.

Opening a review shows the album's cover and current edition, each edition it could be with how many of your files fit it and, per file, whether it fits, doesn't fit or is unsure, and the album's history.

Filter the list by folder, folder policy, identification state or filing date, tick several reviews (or every match of the filter) to keep their tags, use each one's best edition, use one edition they all list ("Use a shared edition" lists the editions every selected review shares, or says there are none), or take them out in one go: you see what will change and what will be left alone before anything is applied. Only the person who made that preview can apply it, within 15 minutes, and if the selected reviews changed in the meantime you're asked to preview again. Applying it starts one background job, and so does retrying many reviews at once: it appears above the list with its progress and can be paused, resumed and stopped. A pause or stop takes effect between albums. A stopped job can't be resumed: start the change again for the reviews still waiting. Each review is checked again when the job reaches it, so one someone changed in the meantime, or (for "use the best edition") an album whose edition someone has since picked by hand, is left alone, and the job says why. Any curator can pause, resume or stop a job. A job that excludes or retries leaves the rest alone if the admin who started it is no longer an admin. Retry identification, on the Scanning tab of Library Management, can be limited to chosen folders or to one sub-folder rule, picked the same way as for a scan. When you approve an edition, DroppedNeedle matches the files to it in the background, as when you choose one on the album page; until that is done the album's files can't be previewed.

## Album organization

When DroppedNeedle groups files into albums wrongly, an admin can fix it from "Album organization" on the album page. Split takes the tracks you tick and makes them a new album (you can name it). Merge folds the whole album into another one you pick. Move puts the tracks you tick on another album. Reset manual grouping hands an album's tracks back to automatic grouping, which undoes a split, merge or move. A new album a reset makes is credited to the artist record a scan of its files would give it. Each one shows a preview first: where every track goes, which albums are left empty, and what happens to each album's edition. Nothing changes until you apply, and if the albums changed in the meantime (a scan ran, someone else edited them) or the preview is more than 15 minutes old, you're asked to preview again. These only change how the library groups your files: no file is moved, renamed or retagged. Later scans keep your grouping. An album left empty folds into the one its tracks went to, so its old links, favorites and playlists still work. Player apps that saved the old album or artist find it too.

Editions follow the album they belong to. An album that keeps its tracks keeps its edition. When an album is emptied into another one that has no edition, its edition moves across. When the albums being combined were matched to different editions, an edition someone chose (or v2 carried over) wins against one identification picked; otherwise you choose whether the receiving album keeps its own or all of them are dropped and the album is identified again; if the receiving album has none and several are on offer, they are all dropped. A dropped edition always comes with a short reason on the album. When an album with an edition someone chose gains tracks, by a merge, a move or a retag DroppedNeedle follows, they are matched to that edition in the background; the edition, who chose it and "leave unmanaged" stay as they were.

After an upgrade from v2, a one-time cleanup tidies album rows the old version left behind: empty album shells are folded into the album they name, and albums that an imported match split away from their siblings in the same folder are put back together. That fold happens only when every file of the split half is tagged with the other album's release; it is listed under "Album history" on the album page, where a curator can undo it ([Library Management](library-management.md#undoing-a-split-album-fold)), and the undo is listed there too. An album the old version left on "Unknown Artist" while its files name the artist takes the artist its tracks are credited to, and is looked up on MusicBrainz again if it had no match. That change is listed under "Album history" too, and a curator can undo it there while the album's artist is still the one it took: the album goes back to "Unknown Artist" and its earlier credits, and the cleanup leaves it there from then on. The same cleanup runs after each scan, finished or not, for the albums that scan touched and the albums it left empty, and after identification finishes with an album. It works in short steps and never while a scan runs. Only the catalog changes, never your files. Artists the old version linked to MusicBrainz keep their link until it is checked again. The upgrade carries each album's match but not the MusicBrainz release details behind it, so the app fetches those in the background while identification has nothing queued, a few at a time, and then checks each artist link against its album's release: a link the release confirms stays, and one it doesn't is removed or replaced. An album whose release MusicBrainz no longer has is identified again, the app stops asking for that release, and its artists keep their links meanwhile. v2 never stored the edition details your tags carry (barcode, catalog number, media, country, disc count, release date), so the first scan after the upgrade reads just those from each file it finds unchanged; nothing else about the track changes. An album among them that has no match, or only a release group or a best guess, is looked up again when its files carry a barcode, catalog number, media, country or more than one disc, the details matching uses; an album a person detached or whose suggestions they turned down is left alone.

v2 matched some albums to a release group without choosing an edition in it. Tagging and organizing need an exact edition, so the album page shows "Edition not chosen yet" for these. After the upgrade, each one v2 matched by itself is identified once more, in the background and only once: it gets the edition its files fit, or a best guess marked unconfirmed, and the automatic choice can be undone from the album page like any other. If nothing fits, it keeps its release group and still reads "Edition not chosen yet". An album whose release group a person chose, in v3 or in v2, keeps it as it is, and albums in folders that keep their own tags are skipped.

Matching now reads the release group your files' tags name. After the upgrade, an album the app matched by itself before that, on a release outside the one group all its files name, is identified once more, in the background and only once. It usually comes back as a best guess marked unconfirmed, saying which album the files name and which the guess is ("Your files say Dummy. This fits Dummy (Deluxe Edition) better."). Albums a person matched or detached, or whose suggestions they turned down, custom editions, and albums in folders that keep their own tags are left alone.

## Artist links

Each artist record can be linked to one MusicBrainz artist. DroppedNeedle links a record by itself when a matched album's release credits it and the record's files don't name another artist, and it moves or removes that link as the matches and files change. Every change to a link, by the app or by a person, is recorded.

The artist page shows the link, who set it and when. A curator can set it by hand with Change, pasting the artist's MusicBrainz ID or page address; an ID MusicBrainz has merged into another is saved as the one it went into. A link set by hand, or carried over from v2, is never changed by the app, except to follow MusicBrainz when it merges that artist into another. Clear removes such a link, and the app links the record again from its albums and files straight away where it can; a link the app set can't be cleared, only replaced. Setting or clearing a link never merges artists: when another record already has that MusicBrainz artist, or its files or matched albums say it is that artist, or when a retag moved the record's songs to another record that would then take it over, the change is refused and names the other records, and an admin merges them under Duplicate artists. The artist link a MusicBrainz contribution sets follows the same rule: when it would join records, the contribution is saved without it. MusicBrainz's special-purpose artists, such as Various Artists, can't be linked.

## Duplicate artists

Duplicate artists can be merged from the artist identity page (admins). The preview counts what moves to the artist you keep: album and track credits, the albums it is the main artist of, favorites, ratings and play history. The merged artist's old links lead to the one you keep. As in earlier versions, a merge you make can't be undone; a join DroppedNeedle made because two records share a name can be (below).

DroppedNeedle also merges two records by itself when MusicBrainz proves they are one artist: every credit of the record that goes is on an album matched to a release crediting that artist, or in a file carrying that artist's ID. A track your files credit to the album's artist alone, where the release credits that artist with guests ("feat.", "&"), counts as crediting that artist. When a retag moves an artist's proven credits to another record, the old record follows them once nothing is left on it, even when a large retag is read in several steps: it merges into the new one, taking its favorites and plays, as long as the album match that proved the credits still stands, unless a play is of a track now credited to someone else or MusicBrainz proves the new record is another artist. Records marked as different people are never merged, and one such pair keeps a whole group of records apart. MusicBrainz's special-purpose artists ("[unknown]", "[anonymous]", "[traditional]" and the like) are never proof: files tagged with one are grouped by name, as if they carried no ID. These merges show as resolved in the duplicate artists list, with what each one rested on, and can't be undone either. When a person chooses or confirms an album's edition, approves a review, or imports an album, its artists are linked and merged right after the change is saved, so they are in place when the page answers; if that step fails, the background check does it shortly after.

Names that differ only in capitals, spacing or the accents of Latin letters ("Beyoncé" and "BEYONCE", "Sigur Rós" and "sigur ros") are one artist unless something says otherwise. A file with no MusicBrainz artist ID goes to the record its exact name and sort name belong to, else to the record of the same name, so a new sort name or other capitals never make a second record; the record takes the new spelling once no file uses the old one. Records of one name that already exist join by themselves at the end of the next scan: plays, favorites and ratings move to the one kept, and the old ID keeps opening it, in the web app and in player apps. They stay apart when they carry two different MusicBrainz artist IDs between them (in a link, in their files or on a matched album), when one of them carries two IDs itself, or when an admin marked them as different people. Each join shows as resolved in the duplicate artists list with an Undo: every record gets back its songs, plays, favorites and ratings, and they are marked as different people so they aren't joined again. What changed since the join stays where it is: a play made after it stays on the record kept, and songs a later scan read again stay where that scan put them. Merges by name from before this release, and merges on MusicBrainz proof, can't be undone. Names that differ in anything else, such as "AC/DC" and "ACDC", or a mark in a script other than Latin, join only on MusicBrainz proof; such records stay listed in duplicate artists. When a retag renames an artist with no proof behind it, and the old record holds favorites, ratings or plays, the list offers it with the records its songs went to, so an admin can merge them or mark them as different people. Where two artists MusicBrainz tells apart share a name, the web app shows MusicBrainz's short note under each name ("US singer"); player apps show the plain name. A retag of a whole library is settled at the end of the scan, and a very large library joins its records over a few scans.

## Albums from the import folder

Albums taken from the [import folder](downloads.md#import-folder) go where downloads go: the first library folder whose policy is Automatic at the path the import naming script gives them, else the first library folder where no file is Excluded. Their covers, cue sheets and logs go beside them. They count as dropped albums for Library Management, so "after dropped folders are imported" on its Automation tab covers them too.

## Removing albums and tracks

Removing an album or a track never deletes your music. The files move into a recycle bin: the recycle bin folder set under Library Management's "Recycle bin, retention and refresh", or a `.recycle` folder at the top of the library folder the files came from. Each file gets its own dated folder inside it. The files that belong with the music go too: the files the folder's Library Management profile moves with an album (cover art, lyrics, cue sheets, logs and playlists, such as `cover.jpg` or `*.lrc`; a new profile's list while no profile is set up). A track takes the ones named like its own file, its lyrics for example, unless another music file of that name stays (removing `01.mp3` keeps `01.lrc` while `01.flac` is there). The rest of a folder's go once no music is left in it, so a folder still holding another album keeps its art. Any other file stays where it is, and so does its folder. Folders a removal leaves empty are removed, up to the library folder, and Put back makes them again. While the files are there, the album's page lists the removal under "In the recycle bin" for curators and admins (when, how many files, sidecars included), and "Put back" returns them: each track is listed again under its own id, on its album, with its plays, favorites and ratings, and the files come back with their permission bits and modified time. This covers removing a whole album, one track, and files not on the edition, including removals made by Discover batches. Put back never replaces a file that has since taken one of their places (a cover art or lyrics file whose place is taken stays in the bin and is named, and the music still comes back), names any file whose copy has left the bin or was changed while in it and puts the rest back (a file edited just before it was removed comes back as edited), and is finished or undone on restart if it stops part way. A removal whose copies the retention has deleted is not offered. If you got the album again while it was out and the new files landed at other paths, putting the old ones back lists those tracks twice; remove the copy you don't want. If they landed at the same paths, Put back refuses because another file sits where a removed file was, and the old files stay in the recycle bin until the retention removes them. A file the duplicates tools sent to the bin is offered on the album it belonged to; when that album has no files left, no page may lead there, and it is put back from the Recycle Bin page. A recycle bin path that is, holds or sits inside a library folder (other than that folder's own `.recycle`), or that overlaps the download staging folder, slskd's downloads or incomplete folder, SABnzbd's completed-downloads folder, or an enabled download plugin's folders, is not used: files go to each library folder's `.recycle` instead, and Library Management shows a warning beside the path. A bin path that is a symlink works; the checks look at the folder it points to. A library folder's `.recycle` that is a symlink into a library or download folder is not used, and neither is one that is a link that can't be followed: removals from that folder are refused and say why, upgrades there are held, and Library Management names the folder in its warning, until it is fixed. While the Library Management, slskd, SABnzbd or download plugin settings can't be read, or the recycle bin set there can't be reached (a share not mounted, a folder the server can't open), removals and upgrades wait rather than pick another bin: removals are refused and say why, and upgrades go ahead by themselves once it can be reached. The app never makes that folder: make it, or mount its share, before you set it. The first time the app uses that folder it leaves a file named `.droppedneedle-recycle-bin` in it; leave it there. A folder at that path without it later (an empty folder where a share isn't mounted) is not taken for the bin, nor, the first time, one holding none of the files the app put in the bin before: removals are refused and say why, upgrades wait, and Library Management shows a warning. Mount the share, and restart DroppedNeedle if it started while the share was down. If you moved the bin, an admin chooses "Use this folder" in that warning, which marks the folder as the bin. After an upgrade from v2, v2's recycle bin folder is taken the first time only if it holds v2's dated folders, which are deleted by their date only once it has been taken; if it was empty, an admin chooses "Use this folder". A library folder's own `.recycle` is made when it is first needed. When the saved Library Management settings can't be read, the Automation tab says so and opens on the starting settings; saving it replaces them, so set the recycle bin folder and your automation again there. A retention the saved settings still hold in range is kept; otherwise the tab shows 30 days, and the warning names the retention a save would apply. Files in either kind of bin are deleted once they are older than the recycle retention (30 days by default, set under the same "Recycle bin, retention and refresh"). While those settings can't be read, nothing in a bin is deleted by age. Admins and trusted users see everything in the bin under Recycle Bin in the sidebar: each removal, or each set of files an upgrade replaced, with its album, how many files, their size, when it was removed and how many days are left. Removals have "Put back" there too; replaced files can only be moved back by hand. Admins also have "Empty recycle bin", which deletes the files now instead of waiting, under the same rules as the retention, after a confirm that says how many files and how much space. It deletes only what was in the bin when the confirm opened, and older dated folders from before the app kept track of them. It runs in the background and the page says what it did; a run that stops is tried again, and the page says when it gave up. A file is kept if it changed while in the bin, if its bin is no longer the one set, or if it is reached through a link. A bin that is, or holds, a library or download folder is never emptied, and nothing is counted as gone while a bin folder holds none of its dated folders (it may be a share that isn't mounted). Removing an album also stops its failed downloads from retrying, drops its held "couldn't verify" files and its blocklist, and stops its wanted watch unless you untick that in the dialog, in which case it starts looking for a replacement. Putting the album back does not undo that: the held files and the blocklist are gone, a stopped watch stays stopped, and a watch that was looking for a replacement settles by itself once it sees the album in the library. A download it already started keeps going; cancel it on the Downloads page if you don't want it. If any file can't be moved, or a file changes while it is being removed, nothing is removed and the page says why. A removal that stops part way (a crash, a full disk) is finished or put back on restart, never left half done. When the recycle bin can't take a file right away, the file waits under a hidden name beside where it was and moves in by itself once it can. Files in the included subfolders of an excluded library folder can be removed too. A library folder whose path goes through a symlink works like any other; links inside it are never followed. A folder reached through a link whose real path is, holds or sits inside another library folder takes no file changes: Settings > Library warns about it, and removals there are refused and say why. Two library folders whose real paths overlap index each shared file once, with that folder's settings: a folder not excluded there (by its own setting or a sub-folder rule) wins over one that is, then the folder that takes file changes, then the one saved first. Settings > Library says so. A file indexed under both before this keeps one entry, and its plays, favorites and ratings move to it. A library folder that isn't there (a drive or share not mounted, or a link to nothing) takes no downloads or file changes until it is back: removals are refused and say why, changes left unfinished by a restart wait for it, and a download that lands while it is away fails as a local fault, so use Retry import once the folder is back. Rescan on an album page rereads just that album's folders. If an album was excluded from file organization, "Manage it again" on its page lets management touch it again.

## Albums with no files

An album with none of its files in the library (removed to the recycle bin, missing from disk, excluded, or with no tracks at all) is not shown in the album lists, on its artist's page, in favorites, ratings or counts, under Check albums, or under another album's Copies. A removed album leaves them as soon as it is removed. Its link still opens it, and it is listed again once its files are back, through Put back on its page or on the Recycle Bin page, or when a scan finds them. Player apps don't show it, and search, Discover and follows treat it as not in your library, so you can request it again. When another copy of the same album has files, and both are matched to MusicBrainz, the album's heart and rating on the copy with no files move to it once nothing of the album can still be put back, and that album's history says so; its plays stay where they were. They stay on the copy they moved to if the old one's files come back.

## Downloading from the library

You can download a single track or a whole album from the library. A track comes as the original file. An album comes as a zip of its files, untouched and uncompressed, and starts downloading straight away, so even a big box set doesn't keep you waiting while the server packs it. Only files the library has scanned, sitting inside one of your library folders, can be downloaded. By default every signed-in user can download; to keep it to trusted users and admins, or admins only, change "Library downloads" under Settings > Security. Streaming is never limited by this setting. The setting is enforced in the web app and on the download addresses of both player-app APIs (Subsonic `download`, Jellyfin `/Items/{id}/Download`), and a player app is told whether its user may download. A player app that saves music through the same address it plays from is not stopped, because the server cannot tell saving from playing. So in player apps the setting holds for apps that use the download address and honor what they are told. It is not a guarantee.

## Lyrics

The web player and player apps show lyrics for a track. Lyrics your files carry come first: a `.lrc` file next to the track with the same name, else the file's lyrics tag. Jellyfin apps such as Finamp only offer lyrics for those tracks. When a file has none, the server asks LRCLIB ([lrclib.net](https://lrclib.net)), a free lyrics database, at the moment lyrics are opened, sending the track's title, artist, album and length. LRCLIB's answer is remembered for a while (a week when it has lyrics, six hours when it has none), so opening the same track again does not ask again. Nothing is written to your files. This is on unless an admin turns it off under Settings > Library > Lyrics; while it is off, the web player and player apps never ask LRCLIB. Library Management fetching lyrics into files has its own setting.

## Ratings

You can give an album 1 to 5 stars on its page, and each of its tracks that has a file in your library. An artist in your library can be rated on the artist's page. Clicking the star you gave clears the rating.

Ratings are kept per account, and they are the same ratings Subsonic apps show and set: a rating given in either place shows in the other. In a Subsonic app, setting 0 stars clears your rating. Subsonic apps also show the average of every user's rating of an item. Once two or more users have rated it, the average can give your rating away to the others: with two users, the other one can work it out from the average and their own. The web app shows only your own.

The player bar shows the heart and stars of the track playing, under its name; on a narrower screen only the heart, and on a phone the heart sits left of Previous. On a phone a queue tab above the bar, beside Close player, opens the queue, and the Listening Room is in the More menu while Local Files is on and not hidden. A Jellyfin, Navidrome or Plex track rates its copy in your library, and one with no library copy shows neither. The Listening Room's deck shows the same heart and stars under the track's title and artist, at every width.

In the player bar and on the deck, the artist and album names link to their pages. An album links when it has a MusicBrainz match, or when it is a local album on a track from your library.

The filter button left of Shuffle skips by rating. "Only play" a number of stars "and up" (2 to 5) makes Next, Previous and the end of a track pass over the queue's tracks rated lower; "Play unrated" (on at first) says whether tracks you haven't rated still play. The button shows the floor, such as 4★+, while it is on, the queue marks the tracks it will pass over "Skips", and "Turn off" plays everything again. A track you pick yourself always plays, and a track with no library copy is never skipped. When nothing left in the queue passes, playback stops at the end of the track and the queue stays, with "No more 4★+ tracks." in the bar. If your ratings can't be read, nothing is skipped and the player says so once. The setting is kept per account in this browser, and phones don't show the button.

The "highest rated" album list in Subsonic apps shows the albums you rated, best first. When albums, artists or tracks are merged, their ratings move to the one that stays; if you rated both, the one that stays keeps its rating. An album rating moves the same way from an album left with no files to the copy of it that has files (see [Albums with no files](#albums-with-no-files)); if you rated both, the copy keeps its rating. Jellyfin apps don't show ratings.

## Favorites

The heart beside the stars adds an album, a track that has a file in your library, or an artist to your favorites; click it again to take it off. To see them, turn on Favourites on the Albums, Artists or Tracks list, or open the Favourites tile on the Library page. The list starts with your newest favorite, and the sort menu still works. On the Tracks list, Play All and Shuffle play only your favorites while the filter is on.

Favorites are kept per account, and they are the same list player apps use: a heart in the web app is a star in Subsonic apps and a favorite in Jellyfin apps, and the other way round. When albums, artists or tracks are merged, their favorites move to the one that stays, and a heart on an album left with no files moves to the copy of it that has files (see [Albums with no files](#albums-with-no-files)). Favorites on a Jellyfin or Navidrome server you linked are that server's own and are shown on its page, apart from these.

## Adding a missing album to MusicBrainz

When an album in your library isn't on MusicBrainz yet, an admin or trusted user can add it from the album page with "Contribute to MusicBrainz". DroppedNeedle never edits MusicBrainz for you; it fills in the MusicBrainz release editor and you check and submit it there, under your own MusicBrainz account.

1. Start the contribution. The draft is built from your files: title, artist, tracklist and lengths. Fix anything that's wrong; every value you change is marked as entered by you.
2. Optionally pick the matching Discogs release. Its label, catalog number, barcode and country can fill the draft, and the Discogs link goes along as a source. Discogs data is only shown for six hours, then you select the release again to refresh it.
3. Run the duplicate check. If MusicBrainz already has the release, link to it instead and you're done. If it only has similar releases, confirm they are different editions.
4. Open the MusicBrainz editor, review and save the release there. MusicBrainz sends you back to DroppedNeedle, which checks the new release in the background (new releases can take a few minutes to show up) and then links the album to it.

If the check can't link the album, the contribution says why and what to do next, and you can retry or enter the release link by hand. If your files change while a contribution is open, rebuild it from the album's current files.

MusicBrainz sends you back to the address your browser used to reach DroppedNeedle, so this works behind a reverse proxy and under `BASE_PATH` without extra setup.
