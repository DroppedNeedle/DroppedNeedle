# Requests and downloads

What happens between pressing Request and the album landing in your library, and what you can do when it does not go to plan. Setting up slskd, SABnzbd, NZBGet and Deemix is in [Download clients](download-clients.md).

## Which edition is fetched

Downloads follow the edition you chose. If the album is already in your library, a request, the edition's "Get the missing tracks" button, or an upgrade fetches the album's edition: the one you picked, else the one that best fits your files. For an album you don't have yet, a request first tries the edition the album page shows, which follows the server's edition preferences (Settings > Editions, set by an admin). It is only a preference: if the files that come in are another edition of the same album, they are accepted, and the album gets the edition the files are. Files tagged as a release of another album are not. The same goes for an album requested from anywhere else, such as a card, Discover or a followed artist. Admins and trusted users can choose the edition instead: the album page shows the edition it will get, and Change opens the list of editions with each one's tracks. Choose one, the one shown included, then Add to Library: only that edition is accepted. You can switch a request to another edition, or make the one it shows your choice, the same way until its download starts; after that the page shows which edition is downloading. Candidates are ranked against that edition's tracklist: first by how many of its tracks a folder holds, then by how few of its files are named for another song, then by how close the track lengths are, then by quality, then (for a whole album) by how few audio files it would download, then by how little other audio the folder holds, then by speed.

A Soulseek file whose number and length fit a track but whose name is another song's title only counts as that track when no file in the folder fits it better, such as one with the right title under a shifted number. A folder with files like that ranks after folders holding as many tracks whose names agree, and "Pick a source" notes the first such file on a folder that otherwise holds the whole edition.

When only one edition is accepted (one a person chose, or the edition of an album you already have) and a Soulseek folder holds every one of its tracks under their own names, only those tracks are fetched, whether DroppedNeedle picks the folder or you pick it under "Pick a source": other audio in the folder is left behind even when it is on your quality list. The folder's other files, such as the cover, still come. A folder that seems to miss a track or fills one with a file named for another song, and a download for an edition that is only preferred, bring the folder's other audio too, in case it is that track or makes another edition of the album whole.

## Single tracks

A single track is fetched the way you would do it by hand. DroppedNeedle looks up which album the track is on (the edition you chose for it if you own that album, else the release you asked from, else the main official album rather than a compilation or a live set), searches for that album, picks the peer sharing the most complete album folder, and downloads only the track you asked for. The file is then checked against that album's tracklist. The song may come from any edition of its album: a file tagged with another release of the same album, carrying its own MusicBrainz recording id, is filed as the release DroppedNeedle looked up. It only adds the song: the album's edition and any "needs checking" mark stay as they were. In a library folder that keeps its own tags, the song keeps its edition's tags, and it still joins the album you have, placed on that album's edition. An upgrade of a song, and a file in a format whose tags can't be written, still need the album's edition. Retrying a song download by hand lifts the album's blocked sources that were refused as another edition (only blocks recorded by this version or later; older ones expire on their own after a week). A peer sharing just the one song is only used when nobody shares the album with it; when that happens the download records why. On Usenet, which can't download single files, the album release is fetched and only your track is imported.

Some songs are on a single that nobody shares, while the album with the same song is everywhere. When the only copy that turns up is the album's track, the download stops at once instead of trying other sources, the file is held, and the request reads "Only the album version was found". Press "Get it from the album" on the request to fetch that track again and import it as the song you asked for; the held copy is then cleared from Held files, unless you are acting on it at that moment. It is only taken when it is the same song: same artist, same title and about the same length as your single's track. The album version can differ from the single (a different mix or edit), so this is your call, not the app's.

The download only stops this way when something besides the file's album tag shows it is the album's track: its own MusicBrainz tags name the album, or MusicBrainz lists a release of that album title by the artist with the song on it. An album tag MusicBrainz doesn't know (a peer's "Singles 2020") counts as a poor match, and the next source is tried. A placeholder album tag such as "Unknown Album" counts as no album tag. Once you chose "Get it from the album", a whole-album download (as on Usenet) gives just the song: its other tracks are left out, neither imported nor held. A copy tagged with the album's own MusicBrainz ids (as Picard tags it) is imported as your single's song too, and AcoustID hearing the album's recording in it counts as a match.

Every finished download is checked before it touches the library: the files must be readable audio of the right album and edition, inside your quality range, and match a release of the album you asked for track by track. Files that pass are tagged (unless their folder keeps its own tags), named by your [import naming script](#import-naming-script) and filed in the library. Files that don't fit well enough are held for you to look at, and bad sources get quarantined so they stop winning.

## Following artists

Follow an artist and DroppedNeedle checks MusicBrainz for their new releases once a day, a few artists at a time and behind your own page loads, so it never crowds MusicBrainz. The first check only remembers what the artist already has. After that, anything new that matches the server's release types (Settings > Release Types, set by an admin) and that you don't already own shows up under Following > New Releases, with a badge in the sidebar. Turn on auto-download for an artist and new releases are downloaded on their release day: one download per release, even when several people follow the artist, and nothing is fetched if it's already in the library or already downloading. Each download is a request of the follower's, so it shows on their Requests page and counts toward their quota; with the quota full it waits there and starts by itself once there is room. Regular users need an admin to approve auto-download first; trusted users and admins don't. An approval stays until an admin revokes it (Requests > Auto-downloads), so turning auto-download off and on again doesn't need a second one. If MusicBrainz can't be reached, the artist is tried again an hour later.

## When a download finishes

DroppedNeedle reads every file, then checks them. Wrong album or edition (a live album or box set when you asked for the studio album; a title spelled a little differently, such as "Rumors" for "Rumours", is not a different album, and neither are files tagged with the album's MusicBrainz id), files outside your quality range, sample clips or unreadable files mean that source is skipped and the next one is tried. Soulseek folders and Usenet releases whose quality the search already shows outside your range are never downloaded on their own; you can still pick one yourself (see [Choosing a copy](#choosing-a-copy)). The rest are matched against the release you asked for, the same way album identification works. With no edition settled, the editions MusicBrainz lists for that album are checked, closest track count first. When a request only prefers an edition, that edition is checked first, then the one the files' tags name if it belongs to the same album, then the album's other editions; files that make up another edition whole go in as that edition rather than as a partial copy of the preferred one. If the album is already in your library, its edition (for "Get the missing tracks" the edition of that copy, else the one you picked, else the one that best fits your files) is the only one accepted, and so is the edition a curator chose for the request: the files are checked against its tracklist, files tagged as another edition send DroppedNeedle to the next source (except for a single track, see [Single tracks](#single-tracks); in a folder that keeps its own tags such a song keeps its edition's tags and still joins your album on its edition), and the missing tracks it asks for come from that edition. A close match goes into the first library folder whose policy is Automatic at the path your import naming script gives it (a path rule there counts), with the full MusicBrainz tags written, and shows up in the library and on the request straight away, without waiting for a scan. Tracks you already have are not imported twice, and extra files the release doesn't list (bonus tracks, scene extras) are left out. Where a file lands in a folder set to Local metadata (the whole library folder or a path rule), it keeps the tags it came with: it is placed and matched, but the release's tags are not written. A track fetched for an album there ("Get missing tracks") joins that album's folder and keeps its tags the same way. If no library folder is Automatic at that path, the download goes into your first library folder where no file's path is Excluded (by the folder or a path rule), and the files whose path there is Local metadata keep the tags they came with; the rest are tagged. A download for an album you already have stays with it: "Get missing tracks", re-requests of that album, upgrades and drop imports don't look for an Automatic folder. They go into your first library folder where no file's path is Excluded, and keep the tags they came with where their path is Local metadata. With several copies of an album, a download joins the copy the album page shows: one with files first (a pick on a copy with no files gives way to it), then the one you picked, then the oldest. When that copy names no edition, no other copy's edition stands in. Files for an edition you already have join that album however they came (a request, a song, an upload, the import folder or a held file you import), even when nothing asked for that copy: they go into its folder and onto its edition, joining the copy whose edition you picked first, then the oldest. Only a copy on that exact edition with files in the library takes them, and only when it doesn't hold those tracks already. A copy on another edition of the album, one whose edition is only a guess or a custom edition, and one with no files left get a separate album, and so does a second copy of tracks you have (a FLAC beside your MP3s). A track fetched for an album goes beside that album's files when they sit in one folder, in whichever library folder holds them, unless that folder is missing, reached through a link into another library folder, or Excluded there. If those files move or go while the download is being placed, everything is put back and it is planned again. Nothing is ever placed under an Excluded path: when every library folder would put a file under one, the download waits with "The import naming script gave no usable path", naming the excluded path, until you change the script or the rules and retry the import. Path rules match folder names whatever their letter case or accents' encoding, so a rule for `Live` also covers `live`; two rules in one library folder that differ only that way can't be saved.

A download is read only from inside the client's downloads folder, and a file or folder below it that is a link is never followed. The folder itself is taken as you set it (`SLSKD_DOWNLOADS_PATH`, the Downloads mount for SABnzbd and NZBGet, or the Downloads folder for Deemix), links on the way to it included, so set it to the folder you mounted into the container, not one below it.

Files with no artist tags go in only when something else shows who they are by: a folder or file name below the client's download folder, or a folder in the path the source shared them under, names the artist (the usual "Artist - Album" folder; for a various-artists release, the album title), the files carry their own MusicBrainz ids, AcoustID recognizes them (with an AcoustID key set, and counting a recording MusicBrainz has since merged into the track's), or the tracklist fits closely (three or more files covering at least 80% of the release's tracks). Otherwise they are held with the reason "The files have no artist tags, and nothing shows they are by this artist." A file whose own artist tag names someone else is held as well when its title or length doesn't fit the track either; a different artist tag alone holds nothing, since classical files often name the composer.

If some files clearly name other songs, sound like a different recording, or the album as a whole is not close enough, those files are copied to the held area for you to look at, that source is skipped, and the next one is tried, asking only for the tracks still missing. A track you asked for in an edition's version ("Replace tracks you have in another version") stays missing until that version comes in, even though you have the same recording in another one. The download settles as held or partly imported only once there are no sources left, or straight away when the only thing in the way is on your side (a file already sitting where a track would go), since another source would not help. The exception is a single whose only copy is its album's track: it is held at once and no other source is tried (see [Single tracks](#single-tracks)). A file sounds like a different recording when, with an AcoustID key set, AcoustID hears another recording in it and its length is more than 3 seconds off the track's (copies of one master are rarely further apart). While AcoustID can't be reached, a download that needs it waits where it is, with nothing counted against the source, and lands by itself once AcoustID answers. If AcoustID keeps answering with an error instead, the download fails after a few tries and keeps its files. An upload matched by itself waits in the held list the same way and is retried by itself; it is checked as a download is, and never replaces a copy you have. An upload that waits for AcoustID with no album yet needs Match.

When a download is an upgrade for an album you already have, a new file replaces your copy of a track only if it is better quality; your old file stays where it is until the new one is in, then goes to the recycle bin, never straight to deletion. If the upgrade stops part way (a restart, a full disk), the old file or the new one is in place afterwards. The one exception: if another program puts a file where your old file was while the upgrade is being undone, your old file is kept under a hidden name beside it and the write is listed under "Some file writes need you" on the Library Management page. If the old file can't be taken away (the recycle bin refuses it, another change is using it, it changed, or it can't be read), the new one is held with that reason, your copy stays, and DroppedNeedle tries again by itself. The new file joins the album the old one was on. In a folder that keeps its own tags, the new file keeps the tags it came with, and your old file, with your tags, goes to the recycle bin. Files that are no better count as already owned: your copy stays and the download's file is not imported. No download or upload replaces a file on an album that tagging leaves alone (an unconfirmed match or "no match", unless "Also manage unconfirmed matches" is on), or on an album with no match whose files' tags name different albums: the better file is held ("A better copy is waiting: this album's match needs checking first") and your copy stays. Nothing replaces your copy by itself, even after the album is checked: a person decides. Curators get "Check this album" on the held card: it opens the album's edition sheet, and confirming or choosing the edition there imports the better file straight away, with "Back to Downloads" to return. Importing the held file yourself replaces your copy if it is better. A wanted album whose only copy in your library is such a guess says so on its card ("You may have this: ... is a guess. Check it"). The background upgrade scan skips these albums. A download with thousands of files is not treated as one album: nothing is imported or copied, and the files stay where they are for you to sort out. Each skip and hold is recorded with a short reason and what you can do about it.

Admins can press Retry import on a failed download to import the files it left on disk right away, with the same checks, without searching or downloading again. A failed download's files are kept for this for 90 days, then cleaned up. Stopping the download cleans them up straight away. For SABnzbd, NZBGet and plugin downloads that means their folder is removed; for slskd only the transfers are cleared, and the files stay in slskd's download folder. Admins can list every request whose download can be reimported this way with the "Can reimport" filter on the Requests history tab.

A download doesn't start while the library folder downloads go to (the first one set to Automatic, else the first one not set to Excluded) is missing (an unmounted share, say), can't be written to or doesn't answer. It stays in the queue as "Waiting: library folder /music isn't there." (or "can't be written to", "isn't answering") and starts by itself once the folder is back. A download that finishes while the folder is gone waits the same way, with its files left where they are, and is imported once the folder is back. Requests are still taken while it waits.

## Import naming script

Downloads and drop imports are filed at the path the import naming script gives them (Settings > Library). It is written in the same naming script language as Library Management, and is read from the tags DroppedNeedle writes, so a Library Management profile with the same script and default path settings never moves an imported file. The default files a track as `Artist/Album (Year)/0103 Title.flac`, with the year left out when the release has none:

```
{albumartist}/{album}{conditional(is_empty(slice(default(original_date, date), 0, 4)), "", concat(" (", slice(default(original_date, date), 0, 4), ")"))}/{disc:02d}{track:02d} {title}.{ext}
```

Every value, function and path rule is in [Naming and tagging scripts](library-management.md#naming-and-tagging-scripts), with recipes and a table for Lidarr's tokens; Settings > Library links to it ("Script reference"). Under the box, the path an example track would get changes as you type, on three sample albums, with blocks to add values and ready-made pieces at the cursor. The script's file name must end in `.{ext}`, and the import always uses the default path settings. Saving checks the script against the first sample. A script that leaves a folder name empty for some album stops that import with the reason "The import naming script gave no usable path": fix the script, then retry the import.

## Held files

Every file that waits for your decision, whether a download, an upload or the [import folder](#import-folder) brought it, is in one list on the Downloads page, and on its album's page. One download or one upload is one entry with its files: an album held from two sources is still one entry. Each file says why it was held and what to do, and each entry says how long it is kept ("Kept until 23 Jan"). Files left in the import folder are kept until you decide ("Stays until you decide"). While nothing is downloading, the Downloads badge in the sidebar shows how many entries wait for you, in amber.

Every entry offers the same actions:

- **Listen** plays a file to hear whether it's the right song.
- **Import** places the files, skipping the check that held each one, but not the comparison with your copy: a file that isn't better than the copy you have is kept, never deleted, and the page says so. Its button then reads **Use this file**: pressing it replaces your copy, which goes to the recycle bin.
- A held file of a lower-quality version (see [Library management](library-management.md)) offers **Use this version**: it becomes your copy, and yours goes to the recycle bin with its plays, favorites and playlist entries moving to the new file. Both buttons say so before anything moves. If your copy can't be taken away, the file stays held with the same button and the reason, so once it can, one press finishes it.
- Files held because something on the library side was in the way (a file already sitting where a track would go, a library folder that isn't there) show **Retry** instead of Import: the library decides again. DroppedNeedle also retries these by itself, a few minutes after they were held and then at longer gaps, up to once a day, until they go in or the entry expires. The entry shows when the next try is, and "Retrying now" while it runs. A retry that comes back with a reason you decide on, such as a file no better than yours, stops retrying. A retry never adds a second copy of a track you already have on another copy of the album, and never replaces a copy that has since turned up on the album itself: that file waits for you instead.
- **Match** (admins and trusted users) picks the album yourself; DroppedNeedle pairs the files with its tracks. A file that would replace a library copy then needs AcoustID to confirm it: when AcoustID doesn't know the file, or can't be reached, the file stays held and your copy stays.
- **Re-check** runs AcoustID again on files it heard as a different recording, could not confirm or could not reach, and imports those it now confirms. While AcoustID can't be reached it says so and the file stays held. "Re-check all" does this across the list, 25 files at a time.
- **Discard** deletes the held files and says how many went. A file the server can't delete stays listed and the page says so. For a download, the album's automatic retry picks up again once none of its files is held.
- A single found only as its album's track offers **Get it from the album** (see [Single tracks](#single-tracks)).

An imported file goes where the download would have put it: a track fetched for an album you have ("Get the missing tracks"), or any file for an edition you already have, joins that copy, in its folder and on its edition; otherwise it goes into the edition you chose for the album, and if the track isn't on that edition, the page tells you to pick the edition it belongs to first. A held download's files keep the tracks matching paired them with, so they can be imported. A file with no track has no Import: an upload's file that was never paired, or a download's file when matching found no release, left it out as an extra or found it too far from its track. Match, for curators and admins, is the way forward. When importing the last held files makes an album whole, its download shows as completed.

Entries are kept for 90 days after they were made, or after their newest file arrived. After that the held copies are removed: only the copies DroppedNeedle keeps for you in its own folders, never a file in your library or in a download client's folder. An entry you are acting on is never removed under you, and a held file whose copy can't be deleted stays listed until it can. Removing an album from the library drops its held downloads too; one you are acting on at that moment goes once you are done with it. If it stays in use for more than a few hours, its copy stays listed until the 90-day prune or until you discard it. If the server stops while an import from the list runs, the files that already went in are marked imported when it starts again, and the rest wait as before.

## Upgrades

With upgrades switched on in the download settings, admins and trusted users see the albums whose worst file is below your quality cutoff and can ask for a better copy of an album or a single track. Asking for one that already meets the cutoff queues nothing. The background upgrade scan, when switched on, works through the same list on its own, under the oldest admin account. It leaves an album alone while files downloaded for it are held for your decision. An upgrade never waits for you to pick a copy: if no copy looks like the whole edition in a quality you accept, it stops with "No better copy was found, so yours stays", and nothing changes in your library. If someone requested the album while the upgrade was looking, it waits for a pick like any request. After an upgrade finishes, the album waits a day before the scan tries it again; from the second failed or cancelled try in a row, each one doubles that wait, up to 30 days. Changing the quality settings does not reset these waits. A copy an upgrade turned down as not better than yours is not fetched again by later automatic tries for that album; the same goes for a copy turned down as the wrong track. Such a copy still shows under "Pick a source", marked "Not better than yours last time" (or "The wrong track last time"), and you can still pick it.

## The Downloads page

The Downloads page updates on its own: progress, new tasks and status changes arrive over the same live connection as the rest of the app, so nothing needs a reload. Every download that failed, was held or landed short shows one short reason and what you can do about it, taken from what the import decided (for example "The files belong to a different album by the same artist"), never a raw error. A download the app stopped because there was nothing left to do (no better copy, already downloading, already in your library) sits in History with your finished downloads, marked Done, and doesn't count as one that didn't make it.

Soulseek track counts include only audio files. Cover art and other extras still transfer with the download, and their bytes count toward progress.

On the Downloads page you can cancel a download, retry it, or move a Soulseek download that sits in a slow peer's queue on to the next source. A Soulseek download that has waited in a peer's queue with nothing downloaded for the preferred-quality wait (15 minutes by default, under the advanced download settings) moves on by itself to another peer's copy, unless it is a copy you picked from a search or the download's last allowed attempt. It only moves once the other copy is queued: if no other peer has the album, it keeps its place and looks again after another wait. The slow peer is not blocked. Stop retrying on a failed download that is waiting to retry stops its automatic retries and its wanted watch. Retry starts a fresh attempt straight away; for an album it also clears that album's blocked sources, so a source that failed before gets another chance. The bulk buttons clear finished downloads, stop every scheduled retry, or retry every failure that won't retry by itself. Admins see and act on everyone's downloads; everyone else sees their own.

Each album has one download at a time, retries included. When a failed download's retry comes due while another download of the same album is running (yours, another user's or an upgrade), the retry doesn't start: the failed download stops with "Already downloading" and its request follows the running one. Pressing Retry then answers with that running download instead of starting a second; while the running download is on your Downloads page, the stopped one offers "See the download" in place of Retry. Retries also stop once the album is in your library some other way (another user's download, an upload or a drop import), with "Already in your library". A download that asked for chosen tracks, or one whose album landed only partly, keeps retrying. A failed download that no retry will look at again, because its retries ran out or a newer download of the same album replaced it, is closed the same way within the hour once the album is in your library, so it stops showing as a failure.

## Choosing a copy

To choose the copy yourself, press "Pick a source" on a failed, cancelled or partial album download. DroppedNeedle asks every download source you have turned on for that album and lists what each one has, grouped by source with the best fit first. Soulseek folders are compared with the tracklist of the edition you asked for, so each one says plainly what it looks like ("Holds all 12 tracks of this edition", "Holds 9 of the 12 tracks", "2 track lengths do not match"). Each copy also shows what it is ranked on: a Soulseek folder its audio format with the bitrate or bit depth its tracks report, a Usenet release its indexer, age and grabs, a Deezer album how many of the edition's tracks it holds and the format it would come in (FLAC 16-bit, MP3 320 or MP3 128), a plugin release the source's own score. Copies outside your download settings (your quality list, the size limit, ignored or required terms, or a plugin score below your minimum) are listed after the rest, each with the reason. Pick one and DroppedNeedle downloads exactly that folder or release, then checks the files like any other download; if it fails, the normal search takes over. Picking a copy outside your settings asks first. Once you confirm, that download is kept even if its format or quality isn't in your settings; every other check still runs, and if it fails, the next source is held to your settings as usual. A copy kept below your quality range may later be replaced by the background upgrade scan. "None of these - keep watching" closes the search and puts the album on your wanted list, and the copies you turned down are remembered so they aren't offered as new finds. A search takes up to a couple of minutes, and the results update by themselves when it finishes. If no source is turned on or none of them answers, the search says so and what to check. The wanted watcher re-searches a watched album the same way: it asks only the sources you have turned on, plugin sources included, with the same filters.

When a Soulseek folder has tracks in more than one audio format, the picker lists each format.

When a Soulseek album search by the full title finds nothing, DroppedNeedle retries with spaces in place of the title's punctuation, such as the dash in a date range. The artist stays in the query, and the folders found are checked against the same edition.

Before downloading an album on its own, DroppedNeedle looks at what every source has, the same way "Pick a source" does. If a copy looks like the whole edition in a quality you accept, it downloads that one. When the edition is only preferred (see [Which edition is fetched](#which-edition-is-fetched)) and no copy fits it, a Soulseek folder that holds every track of another edition of the album, judged the same way, counts too: it is downloaded, and that edition is checked first when the files land. A chosen edition is never swapped this way. Otherwise the album waits in the download queue as "Awaiting review" with the copies it found. A Soulseek folder with none of the edition's tracks doesn't count. You choose one, or press "None of these - keep watching". Nothing is downloaded until you do. Under the default setting, if MusicBrainz could not be reached for the edition's tracklist (or for the edition the album page shows), the copies can't be compared with it, so the album stays queued, shown as Searching, and DroppedNeedle looks again later, waiting longer each time: 15 and 30 minutes after the album was queued, then one, two, four hours and so on. A copy that looks right without the tracklist, such as a Usenet release whose title names the album, is still taken. A day after the album was queued it waits for your pick as above. An edition MusicBrainz doesn't know waits for your pick straight away. You can change this under Settings > Download Client, "Picking a copy": wait only when no copy looks right (the default), always wait for your pick, or never wait and take the best copy found. Upgrades don't wait under either of the first two: they take a copy that looks right, or end without one and keep your copy (see [Upgrades](#upgrades)). Single tracks are not held this way; they are fetched from their album's folder as described above.

## What automatic downloads take

Automatic downloads (a new request, a failover, the Wanted list, an upgrade) take only what your quality list under Settings > Download Client names. That list holds FLAC and MP3 steps, so OGG, Opus, AAC, M4A, ALAC and WAV are never fetched by themselves. The steps count too:

- A FLAC above every FLAC step in your list is left out: on Balanced or Efficient 192+, which hold CD quality, that means 24-bit. A custom FLAC step takes only that exact resolution.
- An MP3 bitrate between your steps is left out.
- A stray bonus file beside a complete album is left behind, and the album is still taken.
- A Soulseek folder with none of the edition's tracks is never taken automatically, even with "never wait"; it still shows under "Pick a source".

A copy outside your list is still shown under "Pick a source", marked "Not in your quality settings". Picking it asks you to confirm, and only that one download skips the check. If only such copies exist, the album waits on the Wanted list and says so. With the Wanted watcher off, it retries up to the limit and then stays failed with that reason. Track requests are not watched.

A search doesn't always show a file's format or resolution. If a download turns out not to be on your list, it isn't added to the library: it goes to the held list for you to import or discard, and the next source is tried. An album keeps one held set of these. Once a complete allowed copy imports, the set is discarded, and that download's line on the Downloads page says so. A copy you import from the held list or upload yourself doesn't discard it.

On Best available, a shown higher resolution beats a faster CD folder, because FLAC ranks by the narrowest step that admits it. In a list you made yourself, order matters: `Any resolution, MP3 320+, CD quality` ranks a 16-bit FLAC after MP3.

## Deezer

Deezer is a download source once Deemix is turned on (see [Deemix](download-clients.md#deemix)). It comes last in the source priority until you move it.

DroppedNeedle looks up the album by its barcode first, on Deezer's public API. A barcode match whose tracks all match the edition by ISRC is taken at once. Otherwise it also searches Deezer by artist and album, reads the tracklists of the closest results through Deemix, and takes the one that fits best.

An album fits when every track of the edition pairs with one of its tracks and it has no extra tracks. Where any edition will do, an album with bonus tracks is taken too, if its title contains the album's title, the artist agrees and its extra tracks come after the rest. Where the edition is fixed (you picked it, your library holds the album in that edition, or the download is for missing tracks or an upgrade), the tracks must match exactly. When more than one album fits, the one whose tracks match best by ISRC and length wins. On a tie, the explicit album is taken.

A song request fetches only its track, from an album that fits. If no album fits, Deezer does not serve the song, and the other sources do.

Quality is the best that your Deezer account can stream and your quality list allows: FLAC, MP3 320 or MP3 128. Deezer's FLAC needs a paid plan and is 16-bit, so it is taken only where your quality list accepts 16-bit FLAC. Not every album has FLAC on Deezer. When one doesn't, DroppedNeedle asks for MP3 320 instead, if your list allows it. When your list allows none of what the account can stream, Deezer waits, and the Deemix card says why.

If a Deezer album arrives partly, the tracks that came in are kept and the rest are fetched from another source.

## The Wanted list

The Wanted tab on the Requests page lists the albums DroppedNeedle keeps looking for. When a watch finds a copy, it downloads the edition chosen on the request, if there was one. The bin on a row removes that watch and the copies it has seen, stopping it first if it is still active. "Clear finished" removes every stopped or found watch you can see; an admin's covers everyone's, and active watches stay. The request stays in your history either way. A removed watch comes back when you request the album again and that fails, when a retry of its download fails, or when you press "None of these - keep watching" on a search for it. Without a removal, stopped and found watches leave the list by themselves after the request history retention.

## Blocked sources

Admins can see blocked sources (Downloads > Quarantine) with the reason each was blocked, and remove one so it can be tried again. Clear all removes every block at once. Blocks expire on their own after a week.

Each block says which check turned the download down and what it found, such as "the download holds no audio tracks". Once a download stops, its line on the Downloads page shows what its last check found, and the server log writes a `download source blocklisted` line with the task id, the check (`code`) and the finding (`detail`). Blocks made by an earlier version show only the general reason.

## Drop imports

Bought music somewhere else? Drop it in. For a whole collection, use the [import folder](#import-folder) instead. Admins and trusted users can upload up to 25 files at once on the downloads page, 8 GB in all: audio files, or `.zip` archives straight from the store. The drop zone shows how much has been sent. If nothing more gets through for a minute, the upload stops with an error. DroppedNeedle unpacks the archives (audio only), treats each top-level folder as one album, and works out which album it is the way a library scan would: MusicBrainz tags first, then the album and artist names (from the tags, or a folder named `Artist - Album`), then AcoustID fingerprints when you have set an AcoustID key.

- A folder that matches closely is renamed with your import naming script, tagged, and added to the library. Where it lands in a folder set to Local metadata, its files keep the tags they came with, and the import's summary ends "Tags kept as they came." If the album is now complete, its open request and wanted watch are settled and everyone who asked for it is told.
- If your library already keeps an edition of that album, the files must match that edition. Files of another edition wait in [Held files](#held-files) instead of quietly importing as a second edition.
- Tracks you already have are skipped as already owned, unless the upload is better quality and clearly the same song: its tags must match the track closely, and with an AcoustID key set the audio must not be a different recording. After a hand match, AcoustID has to confirm it: when AcoustID can't answer (no key set, or the lookup fails), the upload waits for review and your copy stays. Then it replaces your copy, and the old file goes to the recycle bin once the new one is in. The new file joins the album your old copy was on. If the new file can't be placed, or your copy can't be taken away (the recycle bin refuses it, another change is using it, it changed, or it can't be read), your copy stays and the upload waits for review with the reason; it is never deleted, and DroppedNeedle tries again by itself. A file that fails these checks waits for review with the reason.
- Extra files that came with the album but aren't on its tracklist (a bonus track MusicBrainz doesn't list) are kept alongside the album under their own title and number. Files whose tags name a track they don't match wait for review on their own.
- A folder nothing identifies goes in as a local-only album, named by its tags (or a folder named `Artist - Album`), its tags left as they came. It plays straight away, and identification keeps trying by itself (see [How an album is identified](library.md#how-an-album-is-identified)); you can also choose its edition on the album page. It waits instead when its files name more than one album or nothing to name it by, when your library already has an album of that name, release or release group, or when its tags name an album you have that the files don't fit. Such a wait is left for you: it is not tried again by itself, even once that album is gone. An upload held before local-only albums existed, because DroppedNeedle couldn't tell which album it was, gets one more look by itself and goes in as a local-only album if these rules allow.
- Anything DroppedNeedle can't place waits in [Held files](#held-files), shown inside its upload on the Import tab too, with a plain reason and what to do. Pick the album yourself with Match, import files already paired with a track, or Discard to delete them. They are kept for 90 days, like every held file.

An archive with a damaged file, or one that unpacks far larger than it says, is skipped whole: nothing from it is imported, even the files unpacked before the bad one, and the upload names the file. Drop in a fresh copy. Two files in one archive whose names come out the same keep the first, and the upload notes how many were skipped.

If the server stops while an archive unpacks, it starts that archive again from the beginning, so no half-unpacked album is imported. An archive that had finished unpacking is not unpacked twice.

If the server restarts in the middle of an import, the upload's files wait in Held files as interrupted; pick the album with Match or drop them in again.

The server log notes each upload when it starts and when it has arrived (its size and file count), each upload refused as over the size limit, and each import when it starts and finishes.

## Bringing in an existing library

Have a collection DroppedNeedle doesn't manage yet, and want it identified, tagged and filed the way downloads are? Mount a spare folder into the container and set it as the import folder under Settings > Library (the Downloads page's Import tab links there). Then copy a few artists in at a time. Each album goes into your library by itself and leaves the folder, so what is still there is what needs you; copy the next few once it has emptied. If you only want DroppedNeedle to read your library as it is, add it as a library folder instead and scan it.

## Import folder

For a large collection DroppedNeedle doesn't manage yet, set an import folder under Settings > Library: a folder mounted into the container that is not a library folder, the recycle bin, a download folder or one of DroppedNeedle's own folders, and not inside or around one of them (see [Configuration](configuration.md#import-folder)). Copy a few artists or albums in at a time; DroppedNeedle imports them by itself and empties the folder as it goes. Only admins see the setting and what the folder leaves behind.

- An album folder is taken once nothing in it has changed for 3 minutes, so a copy still running is never read half-way. A file or folder whose name starts with `.` is never taken: copy an album as `.Album` and rename it when it's done to start it at once. Disc folders (`CD1`, `Disc 2`) belong to the album above them, and loose files at the top are one more album.
- Albums go in one at a time, the way a drop import does: identified (MusicBrainz tags, then names, then AcoustID), tagged and filed where downloads go (the first library folder set to Automatic, else the first not Excluded). An album nothing identifies goes in as a local-only album, as an upload does. Covers, cue sheets, logs and lyrics that match the library folder's Library Management sidecar patterns (the default list while none is set up) go beside the album. One that would land on a file already there stays in the import folder.
- Nothing is taken while a library scan runs, so a long scheduled scan on a big library pauses it. It also waits, and tries again by itself, while MusicBrainz, AcoustID or the library folder isn't there.
- A file leaves the import folder only once the library holds its exact bytes: DroppedNeedle hashes it again, beneath the folder and never through a link, and removes it only while it is still that file. Folders it leaves empty are removed too. The library copy carries DroppedNeedle's tags; the original is removed.
- An album it can't place (files that name more than one album or nothing to name it by, an album your library already has or whose tags name an album you have that the files don't fit, a file no better than the one you have, an unreadable file, a naming script that gives no path) stays in the import folder and is listed in [Held files](#held-files) as "Import folder", with the reason, Match, Import and Discard. It has no expiry, and holds that the library side caused are retried by themselves. One held before local-only albums existed, because DroppedNeedle couldn't tell which album it was, gets one more look by itself, as an upload does. Discard deletes its files from the import folder; nothing in your library changes. A listed file that changes afterwards (you replace it with a better copy, say) is not acted on as the file you saw: Import, Match and Discard refuse it, and the next pass takes it off the list and looks at it again as new music.
- Library Management treats these albums as dropped folders: "after dropped folders are imported" on its Automation tab covers them.
- Archives are not unpacked. A folder that is read-only imports nothing.
- The setting shows what the folder is doing ("Watching", "Importing", "Waiting for a scan", "Will try again soon") or why it waits ("Overlaps a library folder", naming it). While MusicBrainz, AcoustID or the library folder is away it tries again at growing gaps, up to half an hour.
- Change the folder, or clear the setting, and nothing in the old folder is touched: albums queued from it that had not started stop with that reason, its leftovers stay listed, and files that went in still wait to leave it. Import and Match on those leftovers say the files are in an import folder that is no longer set; Discard only takes them off the list. Set the old folder again and it carries on where it stopped. A folder that can't be reached keeps its leftovers as they are.

A 2,000-album import makes roughly 8,000 to 16,000 MusicBrainz requests, at background priority and at most one a second, so expect a few hours; a person's lookups go first.

### Behind a reverse proxy

A proxy in front of DroppedNeedle has its own upload limits, and a large zip can hit them before DroppedNeedle sees it. An upload the proxy refuses shows an error naming the proxy; one it holds stalls and stops after a minute.

- nginx accepts 1 MB by default. Set `client_max_body_size 8g;` (or `0` for no limit) in the DroppedNeedle `location`, and raise `client_body_timeout`, `proxy_send_timeout` and `proxy_read_timeout` if your uplink is slow. `proxy_request_buffering off;` passes the upload straight through instead of storing it on the proxy first.
- Cloudflare's proxy takes at most 100 MB per request on the Free and Pro plans. Upload larger zips from your own network, at DroppedNeedle's own address, or split the album into smaller zips.

## Indexer limits

DroppedNeedle holds each Newznab indexer to the daily limits it reports. An NZB fetched for a download counts against the indexer's grab limit, and at that limit the download waits instead of failing. Indexers are only called for searches and grabs, never on a timer, so no health check spends a free account's daily API calls. An indexer whose search or caps call fails (it does not answer, or refuses its API key) shows as degraded in the header's status dot until it answers again, or for an hour after its last failure. An indexer that limits a call is rested for a few minutes, longer while it keeps doing so, up to an hour, or for as long as it asks.
